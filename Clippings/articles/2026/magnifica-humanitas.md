***Magnifica Humanitas*** is the first [encyclical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encyclical "Encyclical") of [Pope Leo XIV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIV "Pope Leo XIV"), concerned with "preserving the human person in the age of [artificial intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_intelligence "Artificial intelligence") ". It was published on 25 May 2026.[^1] [^2]

Leo XIV chose to present the encyclical personally,[^3] unlike most other popes who delegated this task to [cardinals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardinal_\(Catholic_Church\) "Cardinal (Catholic Church)"). The presentation was attended by AI experts, including [Anthropic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic "Anthropic") co-founder [Chris Olah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Olah "Chris Olah").[^3]

## Background and release

Since the beginning of his pontificate, Pope Leo XIV made clear that the [rise of artificial intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_boom "AI boom") and crisis of human [dignity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dignity "Dignity") and of [multilateralism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multilateralism "Multilateralism") were major concerns. The encyclical was timed to connect with *[Rerum novarum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rerum_novarum "Rerum novarum")*, his namesake [Pope Leo XIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIII "Pope Leo XIII") 's landmark encyclical of 1891 about [industrialisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrialisation "Industrialisation"), the 135th anniversary of which is marked in 2026.[^4]<sup><span title="Page / location: paragraph 3">: paragraph 3</span></sup>

At the start of May 2026, the Pope approved an Interdicasterial Commission on Artificial Intelligence which would be made up of representatives from seven departments of the [Roman Curia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Curia "Roman Curia"): the [Dicastery for Promoting Integral Human Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dicastery_for_Promoting_Integral_Human_Development "Dicastery for Promoting Integral Human Development"), the [Dicastery for the Doctrine of the Faith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dicastery_for_the_Doctrine_of_the_Faith "Dicastery for the Doctrine of the Faith"), the [Dicastery for Culture and Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dicastery_for_Culture_and_Education "Dicastery for Culture and Education"), the [Dicastery for Communication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dicastery_for_Communication "Dicastery for Communication"), the [Pontifical Academy for Life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontifical_Academy_for_Life "Pontifical Academy for Life"), the [Pontifical Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontifical_Academy_of_Sciences "Pontifical Academy of Sciences"), and the [Pontifical Academy of Social Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontifical_Academy_of_Social_Sciences "Pontifical Academy of Social Sciences"). The commission would "facilitate collaboration and the exchange of information among group members regarding activities and projects related to Artificial Intelligence, including policies on its use within the Holy See, while promoting dialogue, communion, and participation".[^5]

[Anthropic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic "Anthropic") co-founder [Chris Olah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Olah "Chris Olah") was invited to speak at the Vatican's presentation of the encyclical on 25 May 2026. Olah praised the Vatican's role as "informed critics" and the beginning of a "long collaboration between those of us who are building this and those who can see what we, from inside, cannot".[^6] The other experts speaking at the presentation alongside Leo XIV were Professor [Anna Rowlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anna_Rowlands "Anna Rowlands"), Cardinal [Víctor Manuel Fernández](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V%C3%ADctor_Manuel_Fern%C3%A1ndez "Víctor Manuel Fernández"), Cardinal [Michael Czerny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Czerny "Michael Czerny"), [SJ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_of_Jesus "Society of Jesus"), and Professor [Léocadie Lushombo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%A9ocadie_Lushombo "Léocadie Lushombo").[^7]

## Contents

The encyclical is concerned with "preserving the human person in the age of [artificial intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_intelligence "Artificial intelligence") ".[^8] The encyclical particularly condemns the use of [AI in warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_warfare "AI warfare"), stating that reducing human control of weaponry makes it harder to [justify a war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Just_war_theory "Just war theory").[^3] Leo wrote that "The 'just war' theory which has all too often been used to justify any kind of war, is now outdated." [^9] The encyclical also discourages an [AI arms race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_arms_race "AI arms race") and criticizes [deepfakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deepfakes "Deepfakes") in politics.[^3] Leo wrote in *Magnifica Humanitas* that technology is never neutral, arguing that humanity faces a choice between "constructing [Babel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tower_of_Babel "Tower of Babel") and [rebuilding Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Jerusalem "New Jerusalem") "; between remote, prideful power and human-centered community.[^10] [^11] The encyclical also offers apologies for the [Catholic Church's role in slavery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church_and_slavery "Catholic Church and slavery").[^3]

[Tolkien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._R._R._Tolkien "J. R. R. Tolkien") 's *[Return of the King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Return_of_the_King "The Return of the King")* was quoted in the section "We can all do our part":[^12] [^4]

> The twentieth-century Catholic author J.R.R. Tolkien, in the words of a protagonist in one of his novels, described our responsibility in this way: "It is not our part to master all the tides of the world, but to do what is in us for the succour of those years wherein we are set, uprooting the evil in the fields that we know, so that those who live after may have clean earth to till."

## Reception

Margherita Stancati and Sam Schechner of the *[The Wall Street Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wall_Street_Journal "The Wall Street Journal")* described the encyclical as "a text that is poised to define Leo's papacy", saying that it had been "long-awaited" as a useful moral teaching for policymakers and faith groups.[^11] [David Streitfeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Streitfeld "David Streitfeld"), writing for *[The New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Times "The New York Times")*, analyzed the text as representing the contrast between traditional religions and a growing tendency in Silicon Valley to speak of artificial intelligence in quasi-religious tones.[^13] *Magnifica Humanitas* was described by [BBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC "BBC") as "a stark and direct message to those in positions of power about their responsibilities in kerbing the 'threats' it poses".[^3] [Sally Scholz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sally_Scholz "Sally Scholz"), writing in the *[National Catholic Reporter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Catholic_Reporter "National Catholic Reporter")*, praised the document's focus on [solidarity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_social_teaching#Solidarity_and_the_common_good "Catholic social teaching"), saying that it "beautifully synthesizes the tradition".[^14] On the other hand, [Matthew Walther](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Lamp_\(magazine\) "The Lamp (magazine)"), a Catholic writer and AI critic, called it "disappointingly measured and cautious".[^15]
