**Pope Leo XIV** [^1] (born **Robert Francis Prevost**, pronounced [*PREE-vohst*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help:Pronunciation_respelling_key "Help:Pronunciation respelling key");[^13] [^2] September 14, 1955) is the head of the [Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church "Catholic Church") and sovereign of [Vatican City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vatican_City "Vatican City"). He is the first pope to have been born in the United States, the first to hold either [U.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizenship_of_the_United_States "Citizenship of the United States") or [Peruvian citizenship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peruvian_citizenship "Peruvian citizenship"), the first from the [Order of Saint Augustine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Saint_Augustine "Order of Saint Augustine"), and the second (after his immediate predecessor [Pope Francis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Francis "Pope Francis")) from the [Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Americas "Americas").

Prevost was born in [Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago "Chicago") and raised in the neighboring town of [Dolton, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolton,_Illinois "Dolton, Illinois"). He became a [friar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friar "Friar") in the Order of Saint Augustine in 1977 and was ordained as a priest in 1982. He earned a [Doctor of Canon Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Canon_Law "Doctor of Canon Law") (JCD) degree in 1987 from the [Pontifical University of St. Thomas Aquinas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontifical_University_of_St._Thomas_Aquinas "Pontifical University of St. Thomas Aquinas") in [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome "Rome"). His service includes extensive [missionary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missionary "Missionary") work in Peru in the 1980s and 1990s, during which he worked as a parish pastor, diocesan official, seminary teacher, and administrator. Elected [prior general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superior_general_\(Christianity\) "Superior general (Christianity)") of the Order of Saint Augustine, he was based in Rome from 2001 to 2013, and traveled extensively as part of this work, including to the [order's many provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Saint_Augustine#Provinces "Order of Saint Augustine") and [missions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Saint_Augustine#Priories "Order of Saint Augustine") around the world. He then returned to Peru as [Bishop of Chiclayo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishop_of_Chiclayo "Bishop of Chiclayo") from 2015 to 2023. In 2023, Pope Francis appointed him prefect of the [Dicastery for Bishops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dicastery_for_Bishops "Dicastery for Bishops") in Rome, and president of the [Pontifical Commission for Latin America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontifical_Commission_for_Latin_America "Pontifical Commission for Latin America"). Upon his return to Rome, Prevost was made a [cardinal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardinal_\(Catholic_Church\) "Cardinal (Catholic Church)").

As a cardinal, Prevost emphasized [synodality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synodality "Synodality"), missionary dialogue, and engagement with social and technological challenges. He has also addressed issues such as [climate change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change "Climate change"), [international migration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_migration "International migration"), church governance, and human rights. Prevost's election in the [2025 conclave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_conclave "2025 conclave") was unexpected by observers; he was a [dark horse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dark_horse "Dark horse") candidate, with Vatican insiders believing the prospect of a pope from the United States to be unrealistic given its status as a [superpower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superpower "Superpower"). Following the precedent of [Pope Leo XIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIII "Pope Leo XIII"), who developed modern [Catholic social teaching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_social_teaching "Catholic social teaching") amid the tumult of the [Second Industrial Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Industrial_Revolution "Second Industrial Revolution"), Prevost chose the [papal name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papal_name "Papal name") Leo XIV—both to echo Leo XIII's concern for workers and fairness, and as a response to the challenges of [a new industrial revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourth_Industrial_Revolution "Fourth Industrial Revolution") and [artificial intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_intelligence "Artificial intelligence").

As pope, Leo has consistently opposed armed conflict and [nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalism "Nationalism") while advocating for the rights of immigrants. He has introduced new warnings about the use of artificial intelligence technology. Leo has affirmed the teachings of his predecessors, including social teachings on the environment, marriage, and sexuality. He has consistently pointed to the teachings of the [Second Vatican Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Vatican_Council "Second Vatican Council") as the "guiding star" of the church.

## Early life, family, and education (1955–1973)

### Background and ancestry

Robert Francis Prevost was born on September 14, 1955,[^18] [^19] at [Mercy Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insight_Hospital_and_Medical_Center "Insight Hospital and Medical Center") in the [Bronzeville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Metropolis%E2%80%93Bronzeville_District "Black Metropolis–Bronzeville District") neighborhood of Chicago, Illinois, on the city's [South Side](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Side,_Chicago "South Side, Chicago").[^20] [^21] [^22] He is of [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_people "French people"),[^23] [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italians "Italians"),[^24] [^25] [Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spaniards "Spaniards"),[^26] and [Louisiana Creole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_Creole_people "Louisiana Creole people") [^27] descent.[^26] [^20] [^28] [^29]

His father, Louis Marius Prevost, was born in Chicago and grew up in [Hyde Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyde_Park,_Chicago "Hyde Park, Chicago").[^30] Louis's father and mother were immigrants from Italy and France, respectively.[^31] [^32] [^33] He was a [United States Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Navy "United States Navy") veteran of [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II "World War II").[^33] He earned a [Master of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Education "Master of Education") from [DePaul University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DePaul_University "DePaul University") later became superintendent of [Brookwood School District 167](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brookwood_School_District_167 "Brookwood School District 167") in [Glenwood, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glenwood,_Illinois "Glenwood, Illinois").[^34] [^35] [^36] Prevost's mother, Mildred Agnes Prevost ([née](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birth_name#Maiden_and_married_names "Birth name") Martínez),[^37] [^33] [^38] was also born in Chicago, into a mixed-race [Black Creole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Creole "Black Creole") family from [Louisiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana "Louisiana").[^38] [^39] She graduated from DePaul with a bachelor's in [library science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Library_science "Library science") and a Master of Education, becoming an educator and a librarian, including at [Mendel Catholic High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mendel_Catholic_High_School "Mendel Catholic High School").[^40] [^41] [^42]

### Early life and schooling

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/e/ed/Saint_Mary_of_the_Assumption_Church_front_view_cropped_rotated.jpg/250px-Saint_Mary_of_the_Assumption_Church_front_view_cropped_rotated.jpg)

Prevost attended [Saint Mary of the Assumption Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Mary_of_the_Assumption_Church_\(Chicago\) "Saint Mary of the Assumption Church (Chicago)") and its parish elementary school in Chicago as a child.

Known as "Rob" to his family and "Bob" to friends as an adult,[^20] [^43] [^44] Prevost was raised in [Dolton, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolton,_Illinois "Dolton, Illinois"), a suburb bordering the far South Side; [his childhood home](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIV_childhood_home "Pope Leo XIV childhood home") was purchased by the Village of Dolton after he became pope.[^45] He grew up in the [parish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parish_\(Catholic_Church\) "Parish (Catholic Church)") of [St. Mary of the Assumption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Mary_of_the_Assumption_Church_\(Chicago\) "Saint Mary of the Assumption Church (Chicago)") in nearby [Riverdale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riverdale,_Chicago "Riverdale, Chicago"). His parents were practicing Catholics who were very involved in the parish: Louis was a [catechist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catechist "Catechist") —a teacher of the faith—and Mildred was an active member of the Rosary and [Altar Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altar_Society "Altar Society"), sang in the church [choir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Choir "Choir"), provided services for the church, and went to daily [Mass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_in_the_Catholic_Church "Mass in the Catholic Church"). According to Leo, what influenced him spiritually was his parents' faith, prayer life, [Marian devotions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marian_devotion "Marian devotion") ("My parents prayed the [Rosary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosary "Rosary") together their whole lives every day"), and their mutual love.[^46] [^37] His mother would call him and his brothers to go to Mass before school at 6:30 a.m. and told them Jesus "is your best friend," and Mass was "a way to find that friend." [^47] Prevost went to the parish [Catholic school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_schools_in_the_United_States "Catholic schools in the United States"), sang in the choir, and served as an [altar boy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altar_boy "Altar boy").[^20] [^48] [^3] Prevost has two older brothers, Louis Martín and John Joseph.[^50] Prevost aspired to the [priesthood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Priesthood_in_the_Catholic_Church "Priesthood in the Catholic Church") from a young age [^43] and would play-act the Mass at home with his brothers.[^51] He decided to enter the Augustinian order in the eighth grade after visits by priests of multiple different religious orders to the family home, later saying the [Salesians of Don Bosco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salesians_of_Don_Bosco "Salesians of Don Bosco") "came in second place".[^52] [^53]

From 1969 to 1973, Prevost attended [St. Augustine Seminary High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Augustine_Seminary_High_School "St. Augustine Seminary High School"), a [minor seminary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minor_seminary "Minor seminary") near [Saugatuck, Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saugatuck,_Michigan "Saugatuck, Michigan");[^54] [^55] his brother John recalled how, from the end of [eighth grade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eighth_grade "Eighth grade") on, and especially after joining the [Order of Saint Augustine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Saint_Augustine "Order of Saint Augustine"), until their later adulthood when [leave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leave_of_absence "Leave of absence") allowed them to reconnect, Prevost was hardly at home or with their family.[^51]

At the Augustinian seminary, he earned a letter of commendation for academic excellence, consistently appeared on the [honor roll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honor_roll "Honor roll"), served as [yearbook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yearbook "Yearbook") editor in chief, and was secretary of the [student council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Student_council "Student council") and a member of the [National Honor Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Honor_Society "National Honor Society").[^56] [^57] He captained the bowling team and headed the [speech and debate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speech_and_debate "Speech and debate") team, competing in [Congressional Debate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congressional_Debate "Congressional Debate").[^58] [^59] Well-liked and highly intelligent, Prevost had a reputation for helping other students as a tutor.[^60] Out of several dozen who entered the school with him, Prevost was one of only 13 students in his class to graduate.[^61]

## University, seminary, and early priesthood (1973–1998)

### University

In 1973, Prevost was planning to attend [Tolentine College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tolentine_College "Tolentine College"), an Augustinian seminary in [Olympia Fields, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympia_Fields,_Illinois "Olympia Fields, Illinois"), but it closed that same year.[^61] [^62] Instead, he enrolled in [Villanova University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villanova_University "Villanova University"), an Augustinian college located near [Philadelphia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia "Philadelphia"), where he earned a [Bachelor of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Science "Bachelor of Science") (BS) in mathematics in 1977.[^19] [^63] [^64] At Villanova, he took elective courses in [Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew "Hebrew") and [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin "Latin"), read the writings of [Augustine of Hippo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augustine_of_Hippo "Augustine of Hippo"), and discussed the work of theologian [Karl Rahner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Rahner "Karl Rahner") with other students.[^61] He was a founding member of Villanovans for Life, the oldest collegiate pro-life club in the United States.[^65] [^66] He lived in the Augustinian [friary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friary "Friary") and was remembered as "particularly committed... to missionary work" and being "the most community minded" among the students.[^67] While studying at Villanova, Prevost also worked as a cemetery groundskeeper at Saint Denis Catholic Church in [Havertown, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Havertown,_Pennsylvania "Havertown, Pennsylvania").[^68]

### Entry into Augustinians and seminary studies

![Black-and-white head shot of Prevost](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/fd/Photo_of_Robert_Prevost_in_Villanova_yearbook_1977.jpg/250px-Photo_of_Robert_Prevost_in_Villanova_yearbook_1977.jpg)

Prevost in 1977, the same year he entered the Augustinian order

On September 1, 1977, Prevost entered the [Order of Saint Augustine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Saint_Augustine "Order of Saint Augustine") 's [novitiate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novitiate "Novitiate") in the [Province of Our Mother of Good Counsel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_St._Augustine_in_the_United_States#Province_of_Our_Mother_of_Good_Counsel_\(Central/Midwestern_Province\) "Order of St. Augustine in the United States"), residing for one year at [Immaculate Conception Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immaculate_Conception_Church_and_Rectory_\(St._Louis\) "Immaculate Conception Church and Rectory (St. Louis)") in the [Gate District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gate_District,_St._Louis "Gate District, St. Louis") of [St. Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Louis "St. Louis"), Missouri.[^69] [^70] [^71] [^72] In the summer of 1978, Prevost spent three months in [Clinical Pastoral Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinical_Pastoral_Education "Clinical Pastoral Education") at [Abbott Northwestern Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbott_Northwestern_Hospital "Abbott Northwestern Hospital") in [Minneapolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minneapolis "Minneapolis").[^73] [^74] [^75] He took his [first vows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_vows "First vows") on September 2, 1978, and his [solemn vows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solemn_vow "Solemn vow") on August 29, 1981.[^18] [^19]

He returned to Hyde Park, where his father had grown up, to obtain a [Master of Divinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Divinity "Master of Divinity") (MDiv) from [Catholic Theological Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Theological_Union "Catholic Theological Union") in 1982 and taught physics and math at [St. Rita of Cascia High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Rita_of_Cascia_High_School "St. Rita of Cascia High School") in the [Wrightwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrightwood,_Chicago "Wrightwood, Chicago") neighborhood of Chicago during his studies.[^20] [^76] As his [spiritual director](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spiritual_director "Spiritual director"), a guide to the Christian life, he chose a [religious sister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_sister "Religious sister"), Sister Lyn Osiek, [RSCJ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RSCJ "RSCJ"), the supervisor of his theological reflection class. She described him as "calm and steady... a person who was at peace with himself." [^67]

### Ordination and missionary work in Peru

On September 10, 1981, Prevost was ordained a deacon by [Thomas Gumbleton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Gumbleton "Thomas Gumbleton") at St. [Clare of Montefalco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clare_of_Montefalco "Clare of Montefalco") Parish in [Grosse Pointe Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grosse_Pointe_Park "Grosse Pointe Park"), Michigan. That same year, Prevost concluded his studies at Catholic Theological Union.[^77] [^18] Prevost was [ordained](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_orders_in_the_Catholic_Church "Holy orders in the Catholic Church") a priest in Rome, at the [Chapel of Saint Monica of the Augustinians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Monica_degli_Agostiniani "Santa Monica degli Agostiniani") by Archbishop [Jean Jadot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Jadot "Jean Jadot") on June 19, 1982.[^18] [^19] [^78]

Prevost earned a [Licentiate of Canon Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Licentiate_of_Canon_Law "Licentiate of Canon Law") (JCL) in 1984, followed by a [Doctor of Canon Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctor_of_Canon_Law "Doctor of Canon Law") (JCD) in 1987, both from the [Pontifical University of Saint Thomas Aquinas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontifical_University_of_Saint_Thomas_Aquinas "Pontifical University of Saint Thomas Aquinas") in Rome.[^19] During this period, he also learned Italian and took part in the October 1983 protests against the placement of [BGM-109G Gryphon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BGM-109G_Gryphon "BGM-109G Gryphon") missiles at [Comiso Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comiso_Airport "Comiso Airport") which occurred across Europe at this time.[^61] [^79] [^80] [^81] His doctoral thesis was a legal study of the role of Augustinian local [priors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Priors "Priors").[^82]

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/f/f8/Immaculate_Conception_Church_Lafayette_at_Longfellow_St._Louis_MO.jpg/250px-Immaculate_Conception_Church_Lafayette_at_Longfellow_St._Louis_MO.jpg)

Prevost spent his novitiate at Immaculate Conception Church in St. Louis, Missouri.

Prevost joined the Augustinian mission in Peru in 1985, serving as [chancellor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chancellor_\(ecclesiastical\) "Chancellor (ecclesiastical)") of the [Territorial Prelature of Chulucanas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territorial_Prelature_of_Chulucanas "Territorial Prelature of Chulucanas"), as well as an aide to bishop [John McNabb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Conway_McNabb "Juan Conway McNabb"). Much of his work in his first year in Peru focused on disaster relief following severe [El Niño](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Ni%C3%B1o "El Niño") rainstorms.[^18] [^83] In 1987, after defending his doctoral thesis, he was vocation director and missions director of the Augustinian [Province of Our Mother of Good Counsel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_St._Augustine_in_the_United_States#Province_of_Our_Mother_of_Good_Counsel_\(Central/Midwestern_Province\) "Order of St. Augustine in the United States") in [Olympia Fields, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympia_Fields,_Illinois "Olympia Fields, Illinois"), and worked with the faculty of the Augustinian Novitiate in [Oconomowoc, Wisconsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oconomowoc,_Wisconsin "Oconomowoc, Wisconsin"), before returning to Peru in 1988.[^19] [^84] During his time in Peru, Prevost met and appreciated [Dominican priest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Preachers "Order of Preachers") and theologian [Gustavo Gutierrez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustavo_Gutierrez "Gustavo Gutierrez"), a pioneer of [liberation theology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberation_theology "Liberation theology").[^78] He also learned and mastered the [Spanish language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_language "Spanish language") during this period.[^61]

Prevost spent a decade heading the Augustinian seminary in [Trujillo, Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trujillo,_Peru "Trujillo, Peru"), teaching [canon law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canon_law "Canon law") in the diocesan seminary,[^19] serving as prefect of studies, acting as a judge in the regional [ecclesiastical court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecclesiastical_court "Ecclesiastical court"), and working in the parish ministry on the city's outskirts.[^85] [^83] From 1992 to 1999, he was also parochial administrator of Our Lady of Montserrat, Trujillo.[^86] He proved successful in the Augustinians' efforts to recruit Peruvians for the priesthood and leadership positions in the order.[^87] He organized support for Venezuelan refugees to Peru despite discrimination against the Venezuelans.[^88]

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/4/44/Kathedralbasilika_St._Maria%2C_Trujillo%2C_Peru.jpg/250px-Kathedralbasilika_St._Maria%2C_Trujillo%2C_Peru.jpg)

Cathedral Basilica of Saint Mary, Trujillo. As a young missionary, Prevost served in various roles in the Archdiocese of Trujillo.

During the *[Fujimorato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fujimorato "Fujimorato")* era, Prevost criticized the actions of then president [Alberto Fujimori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberto_Fujimori "Alberto Fujimori"), placing special emphasis on the victims of the [Peruvian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peruvian_Army "Peruvian Army"), especially the [Colina Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colina_Group "Colina Group"), during the [period of terrorism in Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Period_of_terrorism_in_Peru "Period of terrorism in Peru"), as well as on political corruption. In 2017, he criticized President [Pedro Pablo Kuczynski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_Pablo_Kuczynski "Pedro Pablo Kuczynski") 's decision to [pardon Fujimori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pardon_of_Alberto_Fujimori "Pardon of Alberto Fujimori") and called upon Fujimori "to personally apologize for some of the great injustices that were committed".[^89] He was described as a defender of the human rights of the population of the [Norte Chico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norte_Chico_\(Peruvian_region\) "Norte Chico (Peruvian region)") region against the violence of the [Marxist–Leninist–Maoist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marxist%E2%80%93Leninist%E2%80%93Maoist "Marxist–Leninist–Maoist") guerrilla organization [Shining Path](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shining_Path "Shining Path").[^90] [^91]

## Prior provincial and prior general (1998–2013)

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/a0/St._Thomas_Church_and_Convent_--_School_02.jpg/250px-St._Thomas_Church_and_Convent_--_School_02.jpg)

St. Thomas School in Hyde Park, Chicago

In 1998, Prevost was elected [Prior Provincial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prior_Provincial "Prior Provincial") of the Order of St. Augustine's [Province of Our Mother of Good Counsel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_St._Augustine_in_the_United_States#Province_of_Our_Mother_of_Good_Counsel_\(Central/Midwestern_Province\) "Order of St. Augustine in the United States") based in Chicago, assuming the role on March 8, 1999.[^19] In 2000, he permitted James Ray, a priest of the Archdiocese of Chicago, to reside at St. John Stone Friary in Chicago under supervision.[^92] Ray had been [suspended from public ministry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suspension_\(Catholic_canonical_penalty\) "Suspension (Catholic canonical penalty)") since 1991 due to credible accusations that he had [sexually abused minors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church_sexual_abuse_cases "Catholic Church sexual abuse cases"). Prevost's allowing of the placement of Ray in a friary at the request of the [Archdiocese of Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archdiocese_of_Chicago "Archdiocese of Chicago") and close to [St. Thomas School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Thomas_Church_and_Convent "St. Thomas Church and Convent") was the subject of reporting in 2021, prior to his appointment to the Vatican.[^93] According to the Augustinians, "the location was selected because of the supervision the priest would receive." [^67] After American bishops implemented stricter rules in the [Dallas Charter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dallas_Charter "Dallas Charter"), Ray was moved to other housing in 2002.[^93] [^94] [^4]

Elected [prior general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superior_general_\(Christianity\) "Superior general (Christianity)") of the Order of Saint Augustine in 2001, Prevost served two consecutive, six-year terms until 2013.[^95] During his tenure as global head of the Augustinian order, Prevost lived and worked in Rome but frequently traveled around the world. It was in that capacity that in 2004, he visited Buenos Aires and met Cardinal Jorge Bergoglio, the future [Pope Francis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Francis "Pope Francis"), for the first time. Their early encounters are reported to have not gone well. Prevost later explained that at the time, he thought he would never become a [bishop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishops_in_the_Catholic_Church "Bishops in the Catholic Church") after Bergoglio was elected Pope in 2013; he has declined to reveal what they initially disagreed upon. However, they were able to reconcile their differences before Prevost left Rome for Chicago in 2013.[^61]

From 2013 to 2014, Prevost served as director of formation at the Convent of St. Augustine in Chicago and as first councilor and provincial vicar of the province of Our Mother of Good Counsel.[^19]

## Bishop of Chiclayo (2015–2023)

![](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/9/9e/Santa_Mar%C3%ADa_Catedral_-_Chiclayo.jpg/250px-Santa_Mar%C3%ADa_Catedral_-_Chiclayo.jpg)

St. Mary's Cathedral in Chiclayo, Peru, where Prevost was diocesan bishop from 2015 to 2023

In 2014, Archbishop [James Green](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Green_\(bishop\) "James Green (bishop)"), [Apostolic Nuncio to Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apostolic_Nuncio_to_Peru "Apostolic Nuncio to Peru"), proposed to Pope Francis that Prevost might be an option for bishop of the [Diocese of Chiclayo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese_of_Chiclayo "Diocese of Chiclayo") in northern Peru.[^96] On November 3, 2014, Pope Francis appointed Prevost as the [apostolic administrator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apostolic_administrator "Apostolic administrator") of Chiclayo and [titular bishop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titular_bishop "Titular bishop") of [Sufar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sufar "Sufar").[^97] Prevost was consecrated on December 12, 2014, at [St. Mary Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Mary%27s_Cathedral_in_Chiclayo "St. Mary's Cathedral in Chiclayo") by Archbishop Green.[^98] [^99] As required by a 1980 agreement between the Holy See and [Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peru "Peru"),[^100] Prevost became a [naturalized Peruvian citizen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naturalized_Peruvian_citizen "Naturalized Peruvian citizen") before being appointed diocesan bishop.[^76] After his naturalization, on September 26, 2015, he was named Bishop of Chiclayo.[^96] [^101] [^102]

At the time of his appointment, Chiclayo was described as a conservative diocese, having been staffed by [Opus Dei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opus_Dei "Opus Dei") priests for many decades.[^83] He entrusted several of them with senior leadership positions,[^103] worked closely with them, and lived with them in the same residence,[^104] calling them hardworking and obedient.[^105]

Prevost developed a close bond with the diocese of Chiclayo, directing a special message to them in his first message as pope, calling them "my beloved diocese", "faithful people", and "faithful church".[^106] [^105] He was seen as being "very faithful to the [Church's social doctrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_social_teaching "Catholic social teaching") " and organized relief efforts during calamities.[^107] He helped victims of trafficking and sex workers learn new skills and trades and invited them to spiritual retreats while ensuring that volunteer workers had adequate training.[^108] He established a diocesan Commission on Integral Ecology and appointed a woman to lead it.[^106]

Prevost was described as a "good listener" and someone with a "disciplined prayer life", joining [Lauds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lauds "Lauds") each morning and the rosary at noon at the diocesan cathedral. He also had great respect for the liturgical rubrics, once correcting a priest who did not wear an alb when hearing confessions.[^107]

On July 13, 2019, Prevost was appointed a member of the [Congregation for the Clergy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Clergy "Congregation for the Clergy"),[^109] and on April 15, 2020, he became apostolic administrator of [Callao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Callao "Roman Catholic Diocese of Callao").[^110] [^5] On November 21, 2020, he joined the [Congregation for Bishops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congregation_for_Bishops "Congregation for Bishops").[^111] Within the [Episcopal Conference of Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Episcopal_Conference_of_Peru "Episcopal Conference of Peru"), he served on the permanent council (2018–2020) and was elected president of its Commission for Education and Culture in 2019, also contributing to [Caritas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caritas_Internationalis "Caritas Internationalis") Peru.[^112] [^113] His presence in the permanent council of the episcopal conference was seen by Cardinal [Joseph Tobin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Tobin "Joseph Tobin") as "extraordinary for someone who was not born in Peru." [^114] Prevost had a private audience with Pope Francis on March 1, 2021,[^115] sparking speculation about a new role in Chicago or Rome.[^116]

Prevost has been accused of covering up [sexual abuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church_sexual_abuse_cases "Catholic Church sexual abuse cases") during his time in Chiclayo.[^117] [^118] In 2022, alleged victims of abuse in 2007 by priests Ricardo Yesquén Paiva and Eleuterio Vásquez Gonzáles said the Diocese of Chiclayo failed to investigate their cases.[^119] The [Diocese of Chiclayo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese_of_Chiclayo "Diocese of Chiclayo") stated that Prevost followed proper procedures, met with Ana María Quispe and her sisters in April 2022 to personally attend the victims, encouraged them to initiate a civil action, and initiated a canonical investigation, the results of which he sent to the [Dicastery for the Doctrine of the Faith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dicastery_for_the_Doctrine_of_the_Faith "Dicastery for the Doctrine of the Faith").[^120] [^121] The sisters stated in 2024 that no full penal canonical investigation had occurred, and an investigation by [América Televisión](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Am%C3%A9rica_Televisi%C3%B3n "América Televisión") concluded that the church's investigation had not been thorough.[^122] [^123]

In response, the diocese of Chiclayo stated that the allegations of the TV program were false, calling on it to correct its inaccuracies. The diocese said the accused priest "never admitted" to the accusations, that Prevost prohibited him from exercising the priestly ministry, and the priest transferred residence to his family's home. Prevost submitted the dossier to the Vatican, which gave a pro nunc, or temporary, decision of dismissal. The diocese later reopened the case and sent it to the Vatican anew, and thus it remains "open up to now." [^124]

Speaking to Peruvian newspaper *[La República](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Rep%C3%BAblica "La República")* while Bishop of Chiclayo, Prevost said: "If you are a victim of sexual abuse by a priest, report it. We reject cover-ups and secrecy; that causes a lot of harm. We have to help people who have suffered due to wrongdoing." [^125] [^126] [^6] Journalist [Pedro Salinas](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pedro_Salinas_\(journalist\)&action=edit&redlink=1 "Pedro Salinas (journalist) (page does not exist)"), who investigated and exposed crimes committed by members of the now-defunct *[Sodalitium Christianae Vitae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sodalitium_Christianae_Vitae "Sodalitium Christianae Vitae")* —including sexual, physical, and psychological abuse—has argued that Prevost always expressed his support for the victims and was one of the most reliable clerical authorities in Peru, leading Pope Francis to select him as prefect of bishops. Salinas has written that some of the Peruvian clerics linked to the Sodalitium sought to attack and defame Prevost in retaliation for his role in the Sodalitium's dissolution by Pope Francis due to its sexual abuse scandals, as well as Prevost's nearness to [Francis's political theology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis%27s_political_theology "Francis's political theology").[^127] [^128]
