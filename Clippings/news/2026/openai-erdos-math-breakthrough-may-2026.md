# OpenAI Solves 80-Year-Old Erdős Geometry Conjecture (May 2026)

## What Happened

OpenAI's o1 model has formally disproved a central conjecture in discrete geometry — a problem that had remained open since Paul Erdős posed it approximately 80 years ago. The breakthrough, published by OpenAI on May 20, 2026, involved using novel deep number theory to demonstrate that the conjecture's central assumption was false.

The discovery has stunned mathematicians. Terence Tao described it as having "ingenious ideas." The result represents one of the most significant applications of AI to pure mathematics in history.

## Competitive Context

A parallel development: Google's Gemini model independently solved a separate 80-year-old math problem, doing so 9-to-1 faster than OpenAI in a comparative test run by The Rundown AI (May 25). This suggests the breakthrough was achievable by multiple frontier models — the era of AI contributing to core mathematical knowledge has arrived.

## Scientific Significance

The specific conjecture involved a long-standing open problem in discrete geometry. o1's proof demonstrated that the conjecture's premise was incorrect — requiring not just computation but genuine mathematical reasoning across number theory and combinatorics.

This follows the pattern of AlphaProof (Google DeepMind, 2024) which solved the international mathematical Olympiad problem. The progression from Olympiad-level to open Erdős-era problems shows AI's mathematical reasoning capability maturing rapidly.
