Complementary properties such as position and momentum cannot be simultaneously well-defined; the experimental arrangement determines which aspect of reality manifests. The observer's choice of what to measure is therefore constitutive of the outcome. Probability here is irreducible and objective: nature itself is indeterministic, and the state vector exhausts what can be said about a system.

## III. The Six Tensions in the Passage

The adversarial cycles surfaced these load-bearing internal tensions:

1. **The Postulation of Collapse.** Quantum theory contains *no collapse operator*. Schrödinger evolution is unitary and reversible. "Collapse" is an additional postulate, not an interpretation of the formalism. Saying collapse is "real, irreversible, and outside unitary evolution" is therefore *not interpretation* but *augmentation* of the theory. The doctrine concedes this implicitly when it requires a "cut" — an external location for non-unitary dynamics.
2. **The Heisenberg Cut is Unanchored.** Bohr refused to fix the cut's location. Von Neumann's chain places the cut arbitrarily anywhere between system and observer, demonstrating that *if* it exists, *where* it is makes no physical difference — which means it is not a physical fact. Yet Copenhagen calls it "fundamental." A fundamental that admits no fact-of-the-matter is not fundamental; it is pragmatic.
3. **The Epistemic/Observational Circle.** A purely epistemic ψ cannot "collapse" because epistemic states do not literally collapse — they are updated. Conversely, a collapse that is real, physical, and irreversible must be a property of something ontic. The passage *needs* ψ to be ontic for collapse to be real, but *needs* ψ to be epistemic for its other commitments. This is the **central circularity**.
4. **Observer Ambiguity.** The "observer" is never specified. Is it a conscious mind (Wigner), an irreversible record (Zeilinger), a decohering apparatus (Zurek), or simply an agent outside the system? Each choice produces a different theory. Copenhagen is **systematically underdetermined at its most critical juncture**.
5. **Probability's Status.** "Irreducible and objective" probability is asserted — but what is irreducible for whom? If the wavefunction is epistemic, probabilities are epistemic; if the wavefunction is ontic, probabilities are ontic. The doctrine cannot have it both ways. Many-worlds eliminates irreducibility at the cost of branching reality; QBism eliminates objectivity at the cost of a single state of the universe; objective-collapse theories (GRW, Penrose) take irreducibility seriously but must specify the dynamics Copenhagen refuses to.
6. **EPR's Sword.** If the state vector is "exhaustive," EPR-Bell correlations demand either action-at-a-distance (Bohr accepted this implicitly) or, per Bell's theorem, a deeper reality (hidden variables). The doctrine's blanket assertion of exhaustiveness places it in tension with every experimental Bell-violation since Aspect (1982).
