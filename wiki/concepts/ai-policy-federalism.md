# AI Policy Federalism

AI policy federalism refers to the dynamic tension between U.S. federal and state-level AI governance, characterized by federal regulatory retreat, aggressive state action (particularly California), and unresolved preemption questions. Unlike the EU's unified AI Act, the U.S. approach is fragmented, creating a patchwork of state-level regulations that increasingly function as de facto national standards.

## The Federal Vacuum

### Trump Administration Retreat (May 2026)

In May 2026, the Trump administration abandoned AI model vetting requirements initiated under the previous administration, signaling a broader federal retreat from AI regulation. Key elements:

- **Executive action**: Formal withdrawal from federal AI safety testing protocols
- **Agency guidance rescinded**: NIST AI Risk Management Framework implementation deprioritized  
- **International signaling**: Absence from Vatican encyclical engagement (only Anthropic co-presented at the Vatican event)

This retreat created a governance vacuum that states — particularly California — moved to fill.

### Historical Context

The U.S. has historically pursued a "light-touch" AI governance approach relative to the EU:
- **2023 Executive Order** (Biden): Established AI safety testing requirements for frontier models — largely symbolic, never fully operationalized
- **No federal AI act**: Unlike the EU, no comprehensive federal AI legislation has passed Congress
- **Sectoral approach**: Existing federal AI governance is scattered across FTC (consumer protection), FDA (medical AI), DOT (autonomous vehicles), and DOD (military AI)

## State-Level Regulation

### California as De Facto Regulator

California's role in AI governance follows the pattern established by its automobile emissions standards (which 17+ states adopted) and consumer privacy law (CCPA/CPRA — which influenced multiple other states):

- **Proposed AI safety legislation**: Multiple bills in the CA legislature requiring safety testing, disclosure, and liability for frontier AI models
- **Compute governance connection**: California's computational resource reporting requirements complement federal export controls
- **Enforcement capacity**: California Attorney General's office and state privacy agency provide enforcement infrastructure that federal agencies lack

### Other Active States

- **New York**: AI bias auditing requirements for hiring algorithms (Local Law 144)
- **Colorado**: AI insurance regulation framework  
- **Texas**: Social media AI moderation transparency requirements
- **Washington**: Proposed AI bill of rights framework

## Preemption Tensions

### The Core Legal Question

Can a state regulate AI in ways that effectively set national standards? The preemption doctrine (federal law overrides state law under the Supremacy Clause) creates uncertainty:

1. **Express preemption**: Federal AI legislation would explicitly override state laws — but no such legislation exists
2. **Field preemption**: Courts might find AI regulation inherently federal (interstate commerce, national security) — not yet tested
3. **Conflict preemption**: State rules that conflict with federal policy (e.g., export controls) would be preempted

### Practical Dynamics

Absent federal action, California's AI regulations function similarly to California's vehicle emissions standards: large-market states set requirements that effectively become national standards because the cost of maintaining separate product lines for one state is prohibitive.
