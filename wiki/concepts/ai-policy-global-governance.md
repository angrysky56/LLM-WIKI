# AI Policy Global Governance

AI global governance refers to the international frameworks, treaties, regulatory institutions, and diplomatic processes that shape how AI is developed and deployed worldwide. Unlike nuclear or biological weapons governance, AI has no dedicated international treaty regime — governance is fragmented across national regulators, voluntary frameworks, and emerging multilateral processes.

## Key Actors and Frameworks

### Vatican/Papal Diplomacy
Pope Leo XIV's encyclical *Magnifica humanitas* (May 2026) established AI as an arms-control/disarmament problem requiring binding international treaties — the first papal encyclical to address AI as a primary subject. The Vatican's 183-country diplomatic network and UN permanent observer status make it a significant global governance voice despite having no direct regulatory authority.

### UN and Multilateral Bodies
- **UN Secretary-General's AI Advisory Board**: Established 2023, seeking common AI governance principles
- **UNESCO Recommendation on AI Ethics** (2021): First international soft-law framework; 40+ countries adopted
- **ITU**: Developing AI standards for telecommunications infrastructure
- **OECD AI Principles** (2019): 40+ countries committed; non-binding

### National Approaches

| Jurisdiction | Approach |
|-------------|---------|
| US (federal) | Retreat — Trump abandoned AI model vetting (May 2026); California as de facto US regulator |
| EU | Binding — AI Act (2024) operationalizes risk-based compliance framework |
| China | State-directed — algorithmic recommendations, generative AI regulations, strategic industrial policy |
| UK | Pro-innovation — Light-touch, no binding legislation currently |
| Japan | Flexible principles, sector-specific guidance |

## Disarmament Framing (2026 Development)

The Vatican's framing of AI as a WMD-equivalent requiring binding treaty controls (rather than voluntary guidelines or ethics frameworks) represents a qualitative shift in global governance rhetoric. Key questions:

- Can binding AI disarmament treaties be achieved given US-China geopolitical fragmentation?
- Does the arms-control framing help or hurt? It elevates stakes but reduces the coalition of willing actors
- Will EU treat the encyclical as validating their AI Act approach?

## Arms Control Treaty Viability

| Obstacle | Analysis |
|----------|---------|
| Geopolitical fragmentation (US/China) | Primary blocker — no current trajectory for joint AI governance framework |
| Verification challenges | AI is software — unlike nukes, can't be counted or inspected remotely |
| Dual-use complexity | Nearly all AI capabilities are civilian + military; scope definitions non-trivial |
| Speed of development | Treaty negotiations take years; AI capabilities evolve faster |
| Enforcement | No international enforcement mechanism for software |
