# Emergent Communication

Emergent communication refers to the spontaneous development of shared protocols, languages, or meaning structures between agents (human, artificial, or hybrid) that were not explicitly designed or taught.

## In Artificial Systems

Multi-agent systems trained with shared objectives develop:
- **Compositional languages** — signals that generalize to novel combinations
- **Efficiency pressure** — compression of redundant signals
- **Grounding** — referents tied to observable states
- **Conversation conventions** — turn-taking, repair, grounding acts

## Relationship to Language Evolution

Emergent communication in artificial agents provides a simplified model for studying how low-bandwidth channels (human language) evolved under pressure for efficiency, expressiveness, and shared reference. The dynamics mirror:
- **Frequency assortativity** — common concepts get shorter referents
- **Taylor's Law** dynamics — burst/punctuated evolution in shared vocabularies
- **Directed Preferential Placement** — new concepts are added near related existing concepts

## Connection to LLMs

As LLMs are trained at scale, they develop implicit communication patterns that:
- Optimize latent representation over human low-bandwidth constraints
- Exhibit topic evolution dynamics similar to natural language
- Can be steered via activation patterns (cf. [[activation-steering]])
