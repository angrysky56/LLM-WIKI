# EU AI Act Implementation

The EU AI Act (2024) represents the world's first comprehensive binding AI regulatory framework, operationalizing a risk-based approach to AI governance. Unlike U.S. federal fragmentation or China's state-directed model, the EU AI Act establishes a unified compliance regime with graduated obligations based on risk category, enforcement mechanisms, and significant penalties for non-compliance.

## Risk-Based Framework

The AI Act categorizes AI systems into four risk tiers with escalating obligations:

| Risk Tier | Examples | Key Requirements |
|-----------|----------|-----------------|
| **Unacceptable** | Social scoring, real-time biometric surveillance in public | Prohibited outright |
| **High-risk** | CV screening, credit scoring, medical AI, critical infrastructure | Conformity assessment, human oversight, risk management system, technical documentation |
| **Limited risk** | Chatbots, AI-generated content | Transparency obligations (disclosure) |
| **Minimal risk** | AI-enabled video games, spam filters | No additional obligations |

### Foundational Model Provisions (2025 Amendment)

An amendment extended the Act's scope to cover general-purpose AI and foundation models, introducing:
- Tiered obligations based on compute threshold (10^25 FLOPs for systemic-risk designation)
- Disclosure requirements for training data
- Copyright compliance obligations
- Mandatory safety incident reporting

## Implementation Timeline

| Date | Milestone |
|------|-----------|
| August 2024 | AI Act enters into force |
| February 2025 | Unacceptable risk prohibitions take effect |
| August 2025 | Codes of Practice for general-purpose AI published |
| August 2026 | High-risk AI system rules for Annex III systems apply |
| August 2027 | Full applicability for all high-risk systems |

## Enforcement Architecture

- **European AI Office**: Central authority within the European Commission, oversees foundation model enforcement
- **National competent authorities**: Each member state designates a market surveillance authority
- **AI Board**: Advisory body coordinating member state implementation
- **Penalties**: Up to 7% of global annual turnover or €35M (whichever higher)

## Vatican Validation (May 2026)

Pope Leo XIV's encyclical *Magnifica humanitas* (May 2026) implicitly validated the EU's approach to AI governance. Key alignment:

- **Binding framework requirement**: The encyclical calls AI regulation a "moral imperative" requiring binding international treaties — consistent with the AI Act's binding approach rather than voluntary frameworks
- **Risk-based framing**: The encyclical's framing of AI as "dual-use technologies" requiring "proportionate regulation" mirrors the AI Act's risk-tier logic
- **Dignity-centered governance**: Human dignity as the foundation of AI governance aligns with the EU's fundamental rights-based approach

The Vatican has permanent observer status at the UN and diplomatic relations with all 27 EU member states, making its validation politically significant — though the EU was already committed to its regulatory trajectory before the encyclical.

## Challenges and Criticisms

### Implementation Gap
- **Member state divergence**: Enforcement quality varies significantly across member states (Germany vs Bulgaria competent authority capacity)
- **SME burden**: Compliance costs disproportionately impact smaller AI developers; the Act's proportionality mechanisms are untested
- **Technical standards gap**: Harmonized standards (CEN/CENELEC) remain incomplete, creating compliance uncertainty

### Global Impact
- **Brussels effect**: Like GDPR, the AI Act is becoming a de facto global standard — companies building for 450M EU consumers apply it globally
- **Regulatory friction**: U.S. federal fragmentation makes transatlantic compliance complex; California's emerging AI rules may align more closely with EU approach than U.S. federal posture
- **China divergence**: China's state-directed approach creates fundamentally different compliance requirements, complicating global AI supply chains
