# EU-US Trade Deal (2026)

## Definition

The May 2026 bilateral trade agreement between the European Union and United States, formally ratified by the EU Council and Parliament on May 20, 2026. Replaces the prior political Joint Statement framework with binding bilateral architecture. Key defining feature: a **2029 expiry/sunset on tariff safeguards**, creating a time-limited window rather than an indefinite arrangement.

## Core Elements

**What was agreed:**
- Tariff elements of the EU-US Joint Statement (reached earlier in 2026) now have implementing legislative/text form
- Council and Parliament reached inter-institutional agreement — signals rapid implementation path
- Conversion from political commitment → legally binding bilateral architecture

**What is NOT finalized:**
- Specific tariff rate reductions and numerical schedules have not been published in publicly available text
- Full implementation text was not accessible at time of writing (EU Council press release blocked by Cloudflare; alternative Commission press release returned no content)
- Official EU Commission press corner publication of implementing text not confirmed

## The 2029 Expiry Mechanism

**This is the structurally novel element.**

The 2029 sunset on tariff safeguards appears designed to address:
1. US constitutional constraints — the Supreme Court's May 2026 ruling in *Hamm v. Smith* invalidated unilateral US tariff authority under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA)
2. Bilateral predictability — time-limited window allows either side to renegotiate rather than locking in permanently
3. Political packaging — frames deal as "not forever" to domestic constituencies skeptical of US-EU deals

**Implication for other bilateral deals:** Malaysia's exit from its US reciprocal trade deal (May 2026) and the collapse of the Rubio-India $500B framework both followed the Supreme Court ruling. The EU-US 2029 expiry format may become a template: bilateral architecture with bounded duration, acknowledging the US can no longer credibly commit to indefinite open-ended tariff arrangements.

## Relevance

- **Transatlantic trade stability**: Businesses on both sides gain defined framework vs. tariff chaos of early 2026
- **Counterbalance to China**: EU-US alignment isolates China in trade negotiations; China publicly warned nations against "appeasing" the US in bilateral deals
- **Geopolitical signal**: Deal demonstrates EU can negotiate bilaterally even when US operates outside WTO dispute settlement
- **Template architecture**: Whether the 2029 sunset-bounded bilateral format replicates to US-Japan, US-ASEAN, and other negotiations is the key open question

## Why Specific Tariff Rates Are Not Available

Three factors preventing authoritative rate data:

1. **Cloudflare block on official EU source** — the Council press release (primary authoritative source) returns 403; alternative URLs also blocked
2. **No official implementation text published in accessible form** — implementing regulations have not been formally published via Commission press corner
3. **Carryover from news cycle** — prior reporting explicitly excluded detailed tariff rate breakdowns ("not available in public reporting")

The underlying tariffs in force before the Joint Statement (US steel/aluminum Section 232 tariffs, EU retaliatory tariffs on US goods) remain partially unresolved. The deal appears to have *structured* the tariff relationship rather than *reduced* the underlying rates.

## Open Questions

1. **Has the full implementation text been published anywhere?** The EU Council press release references attached implementing texts that have not been located in raw/ or Clippings/
2. **Were the specific tariff rates actually reduced, or merely structured/scheduled under the 2029 window?** Current evidence suggests structure over reduction
3. **Does the 2029 expiry mechanism replicate to other US bilateral negotiations?** Japan and ASEAN deals would provides the clearest signal
4. **Are the underlying Section 232 steel/aluminum tariffs still in place?** These were not addressed in the Joint Statement and remain active disputes
