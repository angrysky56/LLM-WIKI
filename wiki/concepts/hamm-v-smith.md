# Hamm v. Smith (2026)

**Hamm v. Smith** is a May 2026 US Supreme Court ruling that invalidated the executive branch's use of the International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA) to impose unilateral tariffs without Congressional authorization. The ruling constrained the Trump administration's tariff authority and reshaped the US bilateral trade negotiation landscape.

## What the Ruling Established

The Supreme Court held that IEEPA does not grant the President unilateral authority to impose tariffs during economic emergencies — such authority requires explicit Congressional legislation. This reversed the prior administration's legal theory that executive emergency powers implicitly authorized broad trade restrictions.

## Impact on US Trade Policy

| Event | Connection to Hamm v. Smith |
|-------|---------------------------|
| EU-US 2029 sunset deal | Sunset format designed to work within new constitutional constraints |
| Malaysia exit from US bilateral | First deal exit post-ruling; Malaysia cited legal uncertainty |
| Rubio-India $500B framework collapse | India walked away after ruling made US commitments unreliable |
| US constitutional constraint | Trump administration now constrained to bilateral deals with Congressional backing |

## Relationship to EU-US Trade Deal

The EU-US Trade Deal's 2029 expiry mechanism was specifically designed to navigate the post-ruling landscape:

1. **Bilateral predictability**: Time-limited window rather than indefinite open-ended commitment
2. **Political packaging**: Frames deal as "not forever" to skeptical domestic constituencies
3. **Constitutional compliance**: Acknowledges US can no longer credibly commit to indefinite tariff arrangements
