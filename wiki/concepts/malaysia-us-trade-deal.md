# Malaysia-US Trade Deal

The Malaysia-US bilateral trade deal was a reciprocal trade agreement that collapsed in May 2026, making Malaysia the **first country to exit** a US bilateral negotiation following the Supreme Court's *Hamm v. Smith* ruling. Malaysia cited legal uncertainty and constitutional constraints on US tariff authority as the reason for withdrawal.

## Context

- Malaysia was among the first tier of US bilateral deal targets (alongside EU, Japan, ASEAN)
- The deal followed the joint statement framework established earlier in 2026
- Malaysia's exit triggered similar withdrawals — India walked away from its $500B framework shortly after

## Why Malaysia Exited

The *Hamm v. Smith* ruling invalidated IEEPA-based unilateral tariff authority. Post-ruling, any US bilateral deal faced:
1. **Legal uncertainty**: US executive could no longer credibly commit to tariff reductions via executive action
2. **Constitutional constraint**: Congressional authorization required — slower and politically harder
3. **Negotiating leverage collapse**: Malaysia (and other nations) recognized US had diminished ability to deliver on tariff concessions

## Template Concerns

The EU-US 2029 sunset format appears designed to address these concerns:
- **Bounded duration**: Commits US to shorter window, easier to authorize
- **Congressional buy-in**: Inter-institutional agreement signals legislative rather than executive-only commitment
- **Renegotiation clause**: Explicit 2029 expiry allows either side to renegotiate rather than lock in permanently
