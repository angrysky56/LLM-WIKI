# Plutchik's Wheel of Emotions

**Also known as:** Plutchik's emotion wheel, psychoevolutionary theory of emotions

## What It Is

Robert Plutchik (1927–2006) proposed a **psychoevolutionary theory of emotion** in his 1980 book *Emotion: A Psychoevolutionary Synthesis*. He argued that emotions are not arbitrary cultural constructs but **adaptive responses shaped by evolution** — organisms at all evolutionary levels face common survival problems, and emotions are the behavioral solutions.

His model identifies **eight primary emotions** arranged on a color wheel (analogous to a color wheel in art), where:
- **Proximity** = semantic similarity (adjacent emotions blend into dyads)
- **Opposition** = bipolar pairs (emotions 180° apart are psychological opposites)
- **Vertical axis (3D cone)** = intensity (emotions range from mild to extreme)

## The Eight Primary Emotions

Plutchik organized the eight primary emotions into **four bipolar pairs**:

| Emotion | Opposite | Survival Function |
|---------|----------|-------------------|
| **Joy** | Sadness | Reproduction / Reintegration |
| **Trust** | Disgust | Protection / Rejection |
| **Fear** | Anger | Protection / Destruction |
| **Surprise** | Anticipation | Orientation / Exploration |

### Intensity Gradients

Each primary emotion exists on a **vertical intensity spectrum** (the 3D cone model). From lowest to highest intensity:

| Low | Medium | High |
|-----|--------|------|
| Serenity | **Joy** | Ecstasy |
| Acceptance | **Trust** | Admiration |
| Apprehension | **Fear** | Terror |
| Annoyance | **Anger** | Rage |
| Pensiveness | **Sadness** | Grief |
| Boredom | **Disgust** | Loathing |
| Distraction | **Surprise** | Amazement |
| Interest | **Anticipation** | Vigilance |

## Dyads: Emotion Blends

Plutchik's key insight: **mixing two primary emotions produces a compound feeling**, analogous to mixing primary colors. He theorized **24 dyads** across three levels:

### Primary Dyads (adjacent — 1 petal apart)

These are the most commonly experienced blends:

| Dyad | Components | Feeling |
|------|-----------|---------|
| **Love** | Joy + Trust | Affection, warmth |
| **Submission** | Trust + Fear | Deference, meekness |
| **Awe** | Fear + Surprise | Wonder, dread |
| **Disapproval** | Surprise + Sadness | Disappointment, shock |
| **Remorse** | Sadness + Disgust | Regret, guilt |
| **Contempt** | Disgust + Anger | Scorn, disdain |
| **Aggression** | Anger + Anticipation | Hostility, assertiveness |
| **Optimism** | Anticipation + Joy | Hope, confidence |

### Secondary Dyads (2 petals apart)

| Dyad | Components | Feeling |
|------|-----------|---------|
| **Envy** | Sadness + Anger | Jealousy, resentment |
| **Sorrow** | Sadness + Fear | Despair, hopelessness |
| **Disappointment** | Sadness + Surprise | Letdown, disillusion |
| **Shame** | Fear + Disgust | Humiliation, self-loathing |
| **Curiosity** | Surprise + Trust | Interest, intrigue |
| **Cynicism** | Disgust + Anticipation | Distrust, suspicion |
| **Pride** | Anger + Joy | Self-satisfaction, triumph |
| **Delight** | Joy + Surprise | Elation, pleasant shock |

### Tertiary Dyads (3 petals apart)

| Dyad | Components | Feeling |
|------|-----------|---------|
| **Anxiety** | Anticipation + Fear | Worry, nervousness |
| **Despair** | Fear + Sadness | Hopelessness, anguish |
| **Guilt** | Joy + Fear | Self-reproach, unease |
| **Sentimentality** | Trust + Sadness | Nostalgic tenderness |
| **Morbidness** | Disgust + Sadness | Dark fascination |
| **Outrage** | Surprise + Anger | Indignation, fury |
| **Dominance** | Anger + Trust | Authority, control |
| **Ambivalence** | Trust + Anticipation | Uncertainty, conflict |

## Plutchik's Ten Postulates

Plutchik grounded his theory in ten postulates:

1. **Animals and humans** — Emotions exist across all evolutionary levels, not just in humans.
2. **Evolutionary history** — Emotions evolved over deep evolutionary time and have a long phylogenetic history.
3. **Survival issues** — Emotions serve adaptive functions for survival (finding food, avoiding predators, mating).
4. **Prototype patterns** — Despite species differences, there are common prototype patterns (e.g., fight, flight, courtship).
5. **Few basic emotions** — Despite the vast vocabulary of emotion words, there are only a small number of *basic* or *primary* emotions.
6. **Others are derivative** — All other emotions are mixed, compound, or derivative states (blends, blends of blends).
7. **Hypothetical constructs** — Primary emotions are hypothetical constructs inferred from evidence, not directly observable.
8. **Pairs of opposites** — Primary emotions come in polar opposite pairs.
9. **Similarity** — Emotions vary in their degree of similarity to one another (hence the wheel).
10. **Varying intensity** — Each emotion varies in intensity or level of arousal (hence the 3D cone).

## The 3D Cone Model

Plutchik's full model is **three-dimensional**:
- **Horizontal circle** = similarity (wheel positions)
- **Vertical axis** = intensity (low at bottom, high at top — like a cone)
- **Depth** = the blending dimension (dyads emerge from adjacent positions)

The cone narrows toward the top (intense emotions are fewer and more extreme) and widens at the bottom (mild emotions are more varied and nuanced).

## Why It Matters

Plutchik's model is one of the most influential frameworks in **affective science** because it:

- Provides a **structural taxonomy** of emotions (not just a flat list)
- Explains **emotion blends** (why we feel "bittersweet" or "anxious-excited")
- Grounds emotions in **evolutionary biology** (not just social construction)
- Maps **intensity** as a continuous dimension (not binary on/off)
- Has been applied in **AI emotion modeling**, sentiment analysis, character animation, therapy, and UX design

## Connections to AI

Plutchik's wheel has been used in:
- **Sentiment analysis** — mapping text to emotion coordinates on the wheel
- **Emotion-aware AI** — modeling user emotional state for adaptive interfaces
- **Character AI** — generating believable emotional responses in NPCs/agents
- **Affective computing** — as a reference taxonomy for emotion classification systems
- **PyPlutchik** — a Python library for visualizing and comparing emotion-annotated corpora using Plutchik's geometry

## Criticisms

- The **rigid 8-emotion taxonomy** may be too restrictive (some researchers argue for fewer or more basic emotions)
- The **wheel geometry** imposes a circular structure that may not perfectly capture all emotion relationships
- **Cultural variation** in emotion concepts is not well accounted for
- The **dyad combinations** are somewhat arbitrary — not all proposed blends are equally well-supported empirically
