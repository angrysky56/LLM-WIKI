# Power as Narrative Compression

The central insight of [[sources/articles/power-as-narrative-compression|source]], formulated as a wiki concept:

> **Power = (control over narrative) / (exposure to counterfactuals)**

This is a *selection-effect* model. Authority figures and institutions do not need to lie or censor to be powerful. They need only curate which counterfactuals ever get a hearing, and the ratio between what is taken as real and what could-have-been-real does the rest. The asymmetry is what gives them power; malice is incidental.

## The Mechanism

Power scales with the asymmetry of access to counterfactuals:

1. The powerful can entertain (and therefore choose among) many versions of "what was, is, will be."
2. The subject encounters one version — the official story — and is forced to act on it.
3. The asymmetry compounds with scope. A person with 10 counterfactuals has a 10× wider action space than a person with 1.
4. The narrative that "wins" is the one whose counterfactuals are invisible to its audience. Inevitability is the tell.

The ratio is, formally, the *information-theoretic* distance between the navigator and the navigated — a [[concepts/compression|compression]] problem with unequal encoders.

## Why Scale Matters

The equation is multiplicative in scale, not just additive:

- Personal scale: a parent with one child holds enormous narrative control because the child encounters few counterfactual versions of "the way things are."
- Institutional scale: a government that controls state media, curriculum, and history education multiplies the effect across an entire population.
- Civilizational scale: a culture that treats its foundational narrative as the only possible narrative ("the end of history" type moves) achieves the same effect at the scale of centuries.

The author explicitly notes: "the larger the scale, the less this can be solved by individual effort, and the more it requires institutional design." This is why [[concepts/institutions]] matter — they are the storage medium for asymmetric counterfactual control.

## The Engineering Insight

Two regimes of thought exist that are sensitive to the ratio:

- **Engineering epistemics** are well-equipped for counterfactuals. A bridge that falls is an unambiguous falsifier; engineers can update because reality forces the issue. This is why engineering (mostly) converges to a working consensus.
- **Economic, political, and other social systems** are not. The "counterfactuals" (other outcomes that could have happened) are not visible by the time the outcome is observed. Asymmetric epistemic cost, not fraud, is the typical failure mode.

This is a structural argument: *no one needs to be lying for the system to be broken.* The very mechanics of social reality, compared to physical reality, produce this asymmetry as a matter of scale.

## The Defensive Move

Defending authority is, mechanically, defending the *singular path* to the current outcome. Any honest "what if" is therefore an attack on the foundation. This is why the powerful often appear brittle and defensive on close inspection: their position *requires* the suppression of obvious alternatives.

The prescriptive note from the source: *deliberate institutionalization of counterfactuals* is the countermeasure. Independent press, opposition, academic freedom, and dissent are not luxuries — they are the load-bearing infrastructure for keeping the ratio bounded.
