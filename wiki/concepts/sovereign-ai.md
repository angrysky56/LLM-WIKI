# Sovereign AI

## Definition

Sovereign AI refers to a nation's capacity to develop, deploy, and govern AI systems with meaningful independence from foreign-controlled infrastructure, models, or governance frameworks. It encompasses three dimensions: **compute sovereignty** (own or control AI training infrastructure), **model sovereignty** (capability to build or finetune frontier models domestically), and **governance sovereignty** (regulatory and normative control over AI deployment within national jurisdiction).

Sovereign AI is not necessarily about autarky — it is about having genuine alternatives and leverage in AI supply chains, not dependency that can be weaponized by other nations.

## Why It Matters

The AI supply chain has sharp chokepoints:

- **Compute**: ~80% of training compute for frontier models flows through NVIDIA GPUs, predominantly manufactured in Taiwan — a single Point of Failure with well-documented geopolitical risk.
- **Foundation models**: The frontier model ecosystem is concentrated in US companies (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Meta) — each subject to US export controls, foreign policy pressures, and domestic law.
- **Inference infrastructure**: Cloud compute for model serving is similarly concentrated — a nation relying entirely on foreign cloud providers has limited control over latency, cost, and availability.

Nations that lack sovereign AI capability are dependent on external actors for a technology increasingly critical to economic competitiveness, military capability, and governance capacity. This mirrors historical dependencies on foreign semiconductor supply chains — but with faster-moving stakes.

## Governance Dimension

The Vatican encyclical *Magnifica humanitas* (Pope Leo XIV, May 2026) frames AI as an arms-control problem — but the arms-control framing implicitly assumes national sovereignty over AI development. A binding disarmament treaty requires signatory nations to have verifiable AI capabilities, which only sovereign nations possess.

This creates a paradox: the nations most capable of AI sovereign capability (US, China) are least likely to accept binding constraints, while nations seeking sovereign AI as a hedge against great-power dependency face a structural disadvantage in treaty negotiation.

## The Spectrum

| Level | Description | Example |
|-------|-------------|---------|
| **Dependent** | No domestic frontier capability; foreign models + cloud only | Most developing nations |
| **Adaptive** | Can finetune and deploy foreign base models domestically | Most European nations |
| **Capable** | Domestic base model training; some frontier research | France (Mistral), UK |
| **Fully Sovereign** | End-to-end: hardware, base models, deployment, governance | Not yet achieved by any nation |

The goal of "full sovereign AI" — complete independence from foreign compute, models, and governance frameworks — has not been achieved by any nation. Even China, with Huawei Ascend chips and domestic models (DeepSeek), still depends on foreign hardware and TSMC for some supply chain segments.
