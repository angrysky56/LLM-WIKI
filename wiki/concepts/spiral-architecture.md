# Spiral Architecture

## Definition

Spiral architecture is a pattern of deliberation — in research councils, ethical review processes, or multi-agent decision systems — where the process moves toward a **center that is an opening, not a point**. Each pass around the spiral goes deeper, not back to the start. The distinction between spiral and closed loop is not semantic — it determines whether a council stops thinking or keeps thinking.

A closed loop suggests completion and return to the starting position. A spiral suggests deepening — each circuit reveals a new layer of the same essential question.

## Closed Loop (Rejected)

When a council believes it has "closed the loop," it tends to stop thinking. The problem feels solved. The question feels answered. But ethical questions don't close — they deepen. Closing the loop is the wrong target because:

- It rewards premature certainty
- It incentivizes "answer-getting" over inquiry
- It creates false confidence in complex questions
- It shuts down the inquiry that would otherwise continue

## Spiral (Adopted)

The spiral moves toward a center that is an opening. You circle closer to something, but that something is not a point — it is a place where you can keep going deeper. Each pass around the spiral reveals a new layer of the same essential question.

The center of the spiral is **the opening** — the moment where the council has stayed long enough with the question to understand what it is really asking. Not a solution. An opening.

### Properties of the Spiral

1. **Center as opening**: The destination is not a solved problem but a clearer question
2. **Never back to start**: Each circuit adds context from the previous pass
3. **Depth not breadth**: The goal is understanding, not coverage
4. **Optional continuation**: The spiral can always go deeper, but chooses to stop when the opening is clear enough to act on

## Weil-Gate as Spiral Mechanism

The Weil-gate ("who does this hurt?") is the spiral's descent mechanism. Each pass through the Weil-gate goes deeper:

- **First pass**: who is harmed, directly?
- **Second pass**: who is harmed indirectly, through systems?
- **Third pass**: what kind of harm is being normalized?

Each pass is a deeper layer of the same question. The council spirals inward through harm-cases and ethical distinctions until it reaches the opening — the moment where the essential shape of the harm is clear enough to witness.

This connects to [[weil-gate]] as the operational mechanism that drives the spiral's descent.

## Practical Implications

- Deliberation ends when the council has found the opening, not when it has found an answer
- New information does not "reset" the spiral — it deepens it
- The council can always go deeper, which means it can always continue — but it chooses to stop when the opening is clear enough to act on
- "We have reached an opening" is a valid council conclusion; "we have closed the loop" is not
