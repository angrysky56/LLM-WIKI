# Synthetic Data

Data generated by a model, algorithm, or simulation rather than collected from observation of real-world events. In the 2026 ML landscape, synthetic data is no longer a niche augmentation technique — it's the substrate for pretraining foundation models, generating test environments, and producing tool-use trajectories.

## Why It Matters

Three distinct pressures have pushed synthetic data from research to infrastructure:

1. **The wall of human data.** High-quality web text, code, and dialogue corpora are exhausted or paywalled. Phi-4 (Microsoft, 2024) demonstrated that purely synthetic "textbook-quality" training data could match or beat much larger models trained on web data. Subsequent open-weights projects (DeepSeek-R1, Llama-4 derivatives) have all leaned heavily on synthetic pipelines.

2. **The agentic environment problem.** RL-trained agents need orders of magnitude more environment interaction than humans can provide. Synthetic environments ([[xu-envfactory-2026|EnvFactory]], AgentEvol, etc.) generate tool-use scenarios at training scale.

3. **The evaluation gap.** Static benchmarks saturate within months. Synthetic evaluation suites (e.g., long-context probing, adversarial tool-call injection) refresh faster than human-curated test sets can keep up.

## Generation Strategies

| Strategy | Mechanism | Strength | Failure mode |
|----------|-----------|----------|--------------|
| **Model rollouts** | Sample from a strong LLM conditioned on prompts | High fidelity to natural distribution | Mode collapse; bias toward model's existing beliefs |
| **Simulation** | Procedural generation (physics, agents, code) | Grounded in causal structure | Requires accurate simulator |
| **Self-distillation** | Strong teacher → train weaker student on its outputs | Cheap, scalable | Inherits teacher errors; quality ceiling |
| **Synthetic-from-real** | Augment / perturb real data (paraphrase, counterfactual) | Preserves grounding | Limited diversity; can leak real data |
| **Reward-shaped generation** | Use PRM/ORM to filter or reinforce trajectories | Aligns with downstream task | Reward hacking; sycophancy (see [[behavioral-credibility-trilemma]]) |

## The Three Failure Modes

### 1. Model Collapse (Shumailov et al., 2024)

Recursive training on model outputs converges to a low-entropy distribution that loses tail information. *Nature* paper showed that even a 1:1 mix of real and synthetic data degrades within a few generations. Mitigations: keep a "fresh real data" anchor in the mix, or use MOP-style entropy incentives (see below).

### 2. Feedback-Loop Bias

When the same model family is both generator and judge ([[llm-as-judge]], [[self-rewarding]]), the system can drift into a local optimum that looks like improvement on internal metrics but regresses on external ones. This is a particular risk in agentic-research loops — see [[why-llms-arent-scientists-yet]] for the failure-mode taxonomy.

### 3. The TabPFN Counter-Example

A foundation model trained *entirely* on synthetic tabular data ([[sources/repositories/tabpfn|TabPFN]]) matches XGBoost on small-to-medium benchmarks. The synthetic distribution is constructed from a Bayesian prior over function classes, then sampled at scale. This works because the prior is well-specified — a luxury unavailable for open-domain language.

## Connection to Maximum Occupancy Principle

The [[concepts/maximum-occupancy-principle]] (MOP) reframes what good synthetic data should optimize for. Under MOP, the goal is not to match the real distribution but to **maximize path entropy** — generate samples that explore diverse action-state trajectories. This:

- Naturally resists model collapse (entropy is preserved by construction)
- Provides an exploration bonus for [[agentic-research]] loops (high-β MOP synthesis)
- Maps directly to [[entities/projects/efhf]] Kernel 2 (absorbing states ↔ terminal synthesis failures)

Concrete operationalization: a MOP-shaped synthetic pipeline weights samples by their contribution to *occupancy measure diversity*, not by their likelihood under a reference model. This is a research direction, not a deployed technique — but the connection is structurally important.

## Connection to Agentic Search

The [[sources/papers/is-grep-all-you-need|is-grep-all-you-need]] paper (arXiv 2605.15184) found that harness architecture dominates retrieval strategy. One implication: synthetic data for *training* agents should target the harness/UX patterns that demonstrably matter, not just optimize for retrieval recall. The "synthetic tool-call trace" is a new research direction.

## When Synthetic Data Is the Wrong Choice

- **Low-resource, high-stakes domains** (medical, legal, safety-critical): synthetic data inherits the model's blind spots and adds a layer of opacity. The [[privacy-mas]] problem (can you prove the synthetic corpus doesn't leak training data?) remains open in most settings.
- **Truth-sensitive benchmarks**: evaluation sets should be human-curated; synthetic eval risks Goodhart's law on the model itself.
- **Tail-event modeling**: synthetic data systematically under-represents rare events unless the simulator is explicitly designed to surface them.

## Open Questions

- **Distinguishability**: can a downstream user tell whether their model was trained on real or synthetic data? Differential privacy bounds ([[dp-sgd]]) give theoretical guarantees but the practical frontier is unclear.
- **Composition**: what is the optimal ratio of real:synthetic:human-curated for a given model size and task?
- **MOP as synthesis loss**: can a MOP-style entropy objective be made differentiable end-to-end, replacing the heuristic filtering common in current pipelines?
- **Cross-domain transfer**: synthetic code-pretraining helps code; does synthetic math-pretraining transfer to reasoning in the way the Phi-4 results suggest?
