# Cognitive Decline

Cognitive decline refers to the progressive deterioration of memory, reasoning, and executive function associated with normal aging and neurodegenerative disease. It exists on a spectrum from age-appropriate forgetting to clinical dementia.

## Types and Stages

| Stage | Characteristics |
|-------|----------------|
| Subjective cognitive decline (SCD) | Self-reported memory issues, no objective deficit yet |
| Mild cognitive impairment (MCI) | Objective deficit on testing,日常生活 intact |
| Dementia | Significant impairment in daily function |

## Primary Mechanisms

### Neuroinflammation-Driven
Chronic low-grade neuroinflammation damages hippocampal neurons and disrupts synaptic plasticity. Microglial activation produces IL-1β and TNF-α which impair memory consolidation.

### Menin-D-Serine Pathway
The 2026 Menin-D-serine-hypothalamus research establishes a specific molecular pathway:
- Menin (hypothalamic protein) declines with age → loss of D-serine synthesis → NMDA receptor dysfunction → impaired memory consolidation
- D-serine supplementation in aged mice improved cognitive performance (3 weeks)
- Physical aging markers (bone loss, skin thinning) were NOT reversed by D-serine — cognitive decline is mechanistically separate from physical aging

### Other Contributing Factors
- Amyloid and tau pathology (Alzheimer's)
- Vascular damage (multi-infarct)
- Metabolic dysfunction (insulin resistance, hyperglycemia)
- Sleep disruption (glymphatic clearance failure)

## Prevention and Intervention

| Intervention | Evidence Level |
|-------------|----------------|
| D-serine supplementation | Mouse models — human trials pending |
| Menin gene therapy | Experimental — not viable in humans yet |
| Anti-inflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) | Mixed — timing critical |
| Cognitive reserve building (education, engagement) | Strong epidemiological |
| Exercise (aerobic) | Strong — reduces neuroinflammation |
| Sleep optimization | Strong — glymphatic clearance |
