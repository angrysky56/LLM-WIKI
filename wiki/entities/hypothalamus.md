# Hypothalamus

The hypothalamus is a small brain region at the base of the forebrain that serves as the body's primary homeostatic control center. It links the nervous system to the endocrine system via the pituitary gland, regulating stress response, metabolism, sleep, temperature, and — according to emerging 2026 research — the pace of biological aging itself.

## Anatomy and Key Nuclei

| Nucleus | Primary Function |
|---------|-----------------|
| Ventromedial nucleus (VMN) | Produces Menin; regulates energy homeostasis and aging |
| Arcuate nucleus | Hormone sensing, appetite regulation |
| Supraoptic nucleus | Water balance, vasopressin release |
| Paraventricular nucleus | Stress (CRH), oxytocin release |
| Preoptic area | Temperature regulation, sexual behavior |

The **ventromedial nucleus (VMN)** has emerged as particularly significant in 2026 research — it is the site where Menin protein is produced, and Menin decline drives the hypothalamic aging switch.

## Homeostatic Functions

- **Endocrine control**: Hypothalamic releasing hormones control pituitary hormone output (ACTH, TSH, LH, FSH, GH, prolactin)
- **Autonomic regulation**: Controls sympathetic/parasympathetic balance via brainstem nuclei
- **Thermoregulation**: Skin vasodilation/constriction, shivering, sweating
- **Hunger/satiety**: Arcuate nucleus senses leptin, ghrelin, insulin
- **Circadian rhythm**: Suprachiasmatic nucleus coordinates sleep-wake cycles
- **Fluid balance**: Osmoreceptors trigger vasopressin release

## The Hypothalamic Aging Switch — 2026 Discovery

The Menin-D-serine research has established the hypothalamus as a central regulator of biological aging:

1. **Menin production**: VMN neurons produce Menin protein, which suppresses neuroinflammation
2. **Age-related Menin decline**: As Menin levels drop with age, microglial activation increases in the hypothalamus and beyond
3. **Downstream effects**: Neuroinflammation spreads, D-serine production falls, cognitive decline begins
4. **Systemic impact**: Bone loss, skin thinning, shortened lifespan — all downstream of hypothalamic Menin decline

Gene therapy restoring Menin in aged mouse hypothalamus reversed aging signs. D-serine supplementation improved cognition but not physical aging markers — demonstrating the hypothalamus controls both but D-serine only addresses the cognitive arm.
