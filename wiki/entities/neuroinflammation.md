# Neuroinflammation

Neuroinflammation refers to inflammation of brain tissue, driven primarily by microglial cell activation. Unlike acute inflammation elsewhere in the body, chronic neuroinflammation is a sustained, low-grade state associated with neurodegenerative diseases, normal aging, and metabolic stress.

## Key Mechanisms

- **Microglial activation**: Resident immune cells of the CNS become chronically activated by trauma, infection, or protein aggregates (amyloid, tau, alpha-synuclein)
- **Pro-inflammatory cytokines**: IL-1β, TNF-α, IL-6 released by activated microglia damage neurons and disrupt synaptic function
- **Blood-brain barrier disruption**: Allows peripheral immune cells to infiltrate, amplifying inflammation
- **Oxidative stress**: Reactive oxygen species produced during inflammation damage neurons

## Link to Menin/Hypothalamus Research

Emerging 2026 research (Menin-D-serine-hypothalamus-anti-aging study) shows Menin — a protein produced in the hypothalamic ventromedial nucleus (VMN) — acts as a suppressor of neuroinflammation in the brain. As Menin declines with age, microglial activation increases, driving cognitive decline independent of other aging pathways. This places neuroinflammation as a downstream effector of the Menin-aging switch.

## Associated Conditions

| Condition | Neuroinflammation Role |
|-----------|----------------------|
| Alzheimer's disease | Microglial dysregulation central to amyloid/tau pathology |
| Parkinson's disease | Alpha-synuclein triggers microglial activation |
| Cognitive decline (age-related) | Low-grade chronic neuroinflammation damages hippocampal neurogenesis |
| Traumatic brain injury | Acute neuroinflammation that can become chronic |
| Multiple sclerosis | Autoimmune demyelination with strong neuroinflammatory component |
