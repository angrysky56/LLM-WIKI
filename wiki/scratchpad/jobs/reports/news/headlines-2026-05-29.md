## 1. Ebola PHEIC — Sustained Escalation, Geographic Expansion, Next Attack Window Open

The Bundibugyo Ebola outbreak in DRC/Uganda continues to escalate with no meaningful containment progress. Three operational factors are converging:

**Case count (May 26 CDC):** ~1,018+ total cases (906+ suspected DRC / 112 confirmed), 244+ deaths. This is a doubling since the PHEIC declaration on May 17. Imperial College London modeling suggests true infections run 2x+ above official counts due to detection gaps in conflict zones.

**Geographic expansion confirmed:** Sud-Kivu province — previously unaffected — reported its first confirmed case (May 26 CDC). The outbreak is no longer confined to Ituri and Nord-Kivu. Major urban centers Bunia and Goma are infected. Uganda has 7 confirmed cases with Kampala transmission confirmed (May 23).

**ETC attack pattern elevated risk:** Three attacks confirmed on Ebola Treatment Centers — May 21 (Rwampara), May 23 (Mongbwalu, 18 patients fled into community), May 26 (Mongbwalu again). The ~2-3 day attack frequency creates an elevated concern window for **May 29-30**. This is the direct mechanism by which official counts systematically under-represent actual spread.

**International response status:** WHO EC temporary recommendations (May 22) are the active governance framework but assume a functioning state health system — conflict zone reality makes full implementation impossible. WHO DG called outbreak "outpacing us" (May 22). No licensed Bundibugyo vaccine exists. NV-387 (NanoViricides) is a pipeline candidate only. Thermostable vaccine deployment decision is still pending.

**Why it matters this cycle:** The combination of (1) case counts doubling, (2) geographic expansion to Sud-Kivu, and (3) the May 29-30 elevated attack window creates a compound risk scenario. The outbreak remains the highest-urgency global health event.

## 2. SpaceX IPO — 13 Days to Listing, Quiet Period Approaching, BlackRock Unconfirmed

SpaceX's June 12 Nasdaq listing is 13 days away. The quiet period — during which the company cannot make new material disclosures — will begin shortly, likely around June 7-10 based on standard SEC practice. The key questions are compressing toward final answers.

**Governance questions unresolved:**
- SpaceX declined to clarify whether Musk's ~78% pre-IPO voting control persists post-listing (24/7 Wall St., May 26)
- Barron's raised Tesla spillover risk — Musk's attention and capital allocation post-IPO could affect Tesla shareholder value
- SEC filing disclosed significant ongoing financial losses and AI space data center as unproven commercial bet

**BlackRock $10B:** Still "considering" with no confirmation as of May 28. The expected disclosure window for an anchor investor is June 7-10 via S-1/A amendment. If BlackRock commits, it will appear there. If not confirmed before quiet period begins, it won't be disclosed until post-listing.

**Pre-IPO milestones hit:** Starship V3 successful test (May 23). S-1 filed and landing confirmed (May 28). $2T valuation target confirmed. Russell/FTSE fast-track inclusion approved. Grayscale: SpaceX to become largest public Bitcoin holder upon listing.

**Why it matters:** This is the largest IPO in history by valuation. The next 13 days represent the final window for material pre-IPO disclosures. The governance question (Musk control) and the BlackRock anchor investor question are the two remaining variables that could materially affect institutional investor pricing on day one.

## 3. California AI Workforce Executive Order — Implementation Phase Active, 60-Day Review Clock Running

Governor Newsom's May 21 executive order mandating a 60-day review of labor policies amid AI workforce disruption is in active implementation. Multiple state agencies are now working on the review — this is the leading indicator being tracked.

**What the order requires:** 60-day review of labor policies across state agencies, preparation of workers and businesses for AI disruption, vendor safeguard provisions for state contracts, novel subsidy mechanism for displaced workers.

**What critics say:** The order creates no mandatory employer obligations, allocates no new funding, and sets no enforcement mechanism. The "protections fall short" framing (CBS8, Crain's) remains accurate as implementation begins.

**Why it matters in the governance landscape:** With federal AI regulation definitively off the table (Trump abandoned AI model vetting before signing, May 22), California's 60-day review outcome becomes the de facto US AI governance framework for workforce protection. The Vatican's encyclical (May 27) and California's implementation are now the two active governance layers in a US vacuum. No US federal official has engaged with the Vatican on AI disarmament framing.

**Next checkpoint:** State agencies must report findings within 60 days of May 21 — meaning late July 2026. The actual policy outcomes (new regulations, funding allocations, mandatory obligations) won't be known until then, but agency actions in the next 2-3 weeks will signal whether the review is substantive or performative.
