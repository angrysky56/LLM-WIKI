## What It Is

A **3-iteration** AGEM run on the 10-distinction philosophy-of-mind corpus (Chalmers' hard problem, P/A consciousness, IIT/GWT, HOT/HOP, predictive processing, qualia/intentionality, type physicalism/functionalism, epiphenomenalism/interactionism, explanatory gap/zombie, multiple realizability/substrate independence). Distinct from [[sources/articles/agem-corpus-1-hard-problem]]'s 2-iter sheaf run, this run **regroups the 10 distinctions into 4 genuine axes plus 2 local technicalities** and surfaces a **layered architecture** (Level 0–3) the corpus implicitly contains.

## The 4 Axes

### Axis I — "What shape would a satisfying answer have to take?"

The deepest axis. The question whether the relevant explanation is **mechanistic-functional** or **metaphysical-modal**. Groups the corpus's most famous disputes:

- **Easy vs hard problem (#1)** — the two sides agree on the shape of functional explanation; they differ on whether that shape can cover subjective experience.
- **Explanatory gap vs zombie argument (#9)** — same axis internal to the anti-physicalist camp. Levine = epistemic humility, Chalmers = positive metaphysical claim.
- **Epiphenomenalism vs interactionist dualism (#8)** — the axis applied to the causal question. If phenomenal states are inert, the hard problem becomes academic. If they grip the wheel, phenomenal properties must enter causal accounts.

These three pairs are the *same axis* in different domains: shape of explanation (1), confidence about explanation (9), and whether the explanandum is even causally relevant (8).

### Axis II — "Intrinsic vs relational property"

Explicitly flagged in pair #3 (IIT vs GWT), but reaches further:

- **IIT (Φ) vs GWT (broadcast) (#3)** — Φ is intrinsic (system's own cause-effect structure), broadcast is relational (made available to many subsystems).
- **Phenomenal vs access consciousness (#2)** — P is intrinsic experiential character, A is relational availability to reasoning, reporting, control.
- **Qualia vs intentionality (#6)** — qualia are felt from the inside (intrinsic); intentionality is about something (relational to a represented object).

These split on the same polarity: is the relevant property something the system has *in itself*, or something it stands in *relation to* other things?

### Axis III — "What makes the kind vs what makes the phenomenon"

Most explicit in pair #10; pair #7 is its upstream cousin:

- **Multiple realizability vs substrate independence (#10)** — MR is a claim about *kinds*; SI is a claim about the *phenomenon* of consciousness.
- **Type physicalism vs functionalism (#7)** — type physicalism says a mental *kind* is identical to a physical kind; functionalism defines by causal role, opening the door to MR.

The non-obvious move: you can be a type physicalist who believes the kind has a single physical realizer *and* think consciousness as a phenomenon is substrate-independent in some exotic sense. The axes overlap but the questions are different.

### Axis IV — "What kind of higher-order structure is involved"

- **HOT vs HOP (#4)** — both accept meta-state; they divide on thought-like (HOT) vs perception-like (HOP).
- **Prediction error vs precision (#5)** — the most local of the 10 pairs. Error is content; precision is gain. They live in different formalisms in cognitive neuroscience.

Axis-IV is "what does the formalism need to keep distinct" — same kind of question as the others, at a different level of resolution.

## Three Deceptive Surface Similarities the Corpus Warns About

1. **Easy/hard (#1) and explanatory gap/zombie (#9) both look "epistemic-vs-modal"** — but easy/hard is about what an *explanation* would look like; gap/zombie is about whether the *world* is such that an explanation could in principle close. The graph cluster #3 actually fuses them, which is exactly the move the corpus warns against.
2. **IIT/GWT (#3) and P/A (#2) both look "intrinsic vs relational"** — but IIT/GWT asks *where* consciousness is located; P/A asks *what* a conscious state consists in. You could be an IIT theorist who holds that Φ *is* what gets globally broadcast (a hybrid); the categories cut across each other.
3. **Type physicalism/functionalism (#7) and MR/SI (#10) both look "substrate-related"** — but (7) is the *metaphysical* question of what makes a kind; (10) is the *engineering* question of what preserves a phenomenon. A functionalist need not believe in mind-uploading.

## The Layered Architecture (the Deeper Pattern)

If you read the corpus as a layered argument:

1. **Level 0 — what is at stake?** Axis I asks whether phenomenal consciousness can be captured in the kind of explanation cognitive science already uses.
2. **Level 1 — what kind of thing is it?** Axes II and III ask what *category of property* consciousness is: intrinsic or relational (II), bound to substrate or to pattern (III)?
3. **Level 2 — what does a specific theory say?** Within whichever verdicts Level 1 commits you to, IIT vs GWT, functionalism vs type identity, representationalism vs raw-feeling theories of qualia.
4. **Level 3 — what does the formalism need?** Axis IV and the technical pairs (HOT/HOP, error/precision) are intra-theoretic distinctions.

**The corpus's most precise message is that summaries collapse across these levels.** They say "consciousness is information integration" (Level 2 verdict) and erase the Level 3 fight between IIT and GWT. They say "the mind is physical" (Axis III *kind* claim) and slip in substrate independence (Axis III *phenomenon* claim). They treat Block's P/A as a refinement of Chalmers' easy/hard (Level 0) when in fact they operate at different levels.

## What the Engine Did and Did Not Do

The graph supports the regrouping. H⁰=1, modularity ≈ 0.59, bridge structure all indicate the corpus holds together as a single semantic component with well-separated sub-topics — exactly what the layered architecture predicts. No H¹ obstruction was reported, consistent with the corpus being a *consistent* set of warnings about other people's summaries.

**Caveat**: the "are these two distinctions really the same axis" verdicts in section 2 of the source are interpretive, not direct readouts from the graph. The graph tells you which distinctions *co-occur* in the corpus; it does not adjudicate whether co-occurrence reflects shared structure or shared vocabulary. That adjudication is the analyst's; a structural engine alone cannot make it.
