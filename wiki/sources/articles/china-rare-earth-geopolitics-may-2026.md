# China Against Everybody: The New Geopolitics of Rare Earth Elements

## What Happened

China has moved to consolidate its dominance over rare earth elements as a geopolitical lever, restricting global access to these critical materials amid escalating US-China technology tensions, 24/7 Wall St. reported (May 24, 2026). The piece documents how China's near-monopoly on rare earth mining, processing, and refining has become a core component of its geopolitical strategy against what it characterizes as US-led containment efforts.

Rare earth elements (REEs) — 17 minerals including lanthanum, cerium, and neodymium — are essential inputs for electric vehicles, wind turbines, defense systems, and advanced electronics. China controls approximately 60-70% of global rare earth mining capacity and near-100% of rare earth processing and refining.

The article outlines how China's rare earth leverage has been deployed asymmetrically: restricting exports to companies and countries that take positions contrary to Chinese interests, while offering preferential access to those who align with Belt and Road Initiative priorities.

## Why It Matters

1. **Critical dependency for Western technology** — The US, EU, and allies have limited ability to scale rare earth production outside China in the near term (5-10 year lead time for new mines), creating a structural vulnerability.

2. **Defense industrial base at risk** — F-35 fighter jets, naval systems, and advanced munitions require rare earths; China has explicitly signaled it could restrict these in a Taiwan conflict scenario.

3. **EV and clean energy transition implications** — Permanent magnet motors in EVs and wind turbines depend on neodymium and praseodymium; supply disruption would meaningfully slow decarbonization timelines.

4. **Precedent for weaponizing resource dominance** — China's rare earth strategy serves as a template for how resource dominance can substitute for military deterrence in great power competition.

5. **US responses inadequate** — The US has attempted to build stockpiles, fund allied mining projects, and accelerate processing facility construction, but none has reached scale as of May 2026.
