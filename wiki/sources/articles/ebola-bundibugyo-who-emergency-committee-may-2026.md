# Ebola Bundibugyo — WHO Emergency Committee Temporary Recommendations (May 2026)

## Summary

The WHO's IHR (International Health Regulations) Emergency Committee convened on **May 22, 2026** regarding the Ebola Bundibugyo virus disease outbreak in the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC) and Uganda. This is the first formal international governance escalation mechanism triggered under the PHEIC declared earlier. The Committee issued temporary recommendations covering border monitoring, vaccine sharing, and coordinated resource mobilization.

## Key Facts

- **Strain:** Bundibugyo Ebola virus (Ebola virus species) — rare, **no approved treatment or vaccine** at time of outbreak
- **Scale (May 22):** ~750 suspected cases, 177 deaths (CIDRAP report, May 22)
- **WHO Director-General (May 22 briefing):** Outbreak is "outpacing us"
- **Risk level:** Area risk upgraded to "very high" 
- **Recommendations:** Temporary recommendations issued by IHR EC — specifics include coordinated border health screening, accelerated vaccine/therapeutic sharing, and enhanced surveillance in conflict zones
- **Thermostable vaccine technology** flagged for potential deployment (would be significant for conflict-zone logistics where cold-chain is unreliable)
- **NanoViricides NV-387** (oral broad-spectrum antiviral) flagged as pipeline candidate — no approved counterpart for Bundibugyo

## Context

The outbreak is concentrated in eastern DRC, in territory controlled by armed groups. 18+ patients fled into rebel-held areas after ETC (Ebola Treatment Centre) attacks on May 20, 23, and 26 — making official case counts systematically underrepresent actual spread. The Bundibugyo strain's absence from approved countermeasure lists makes this outbreak qualitatively more dangerous than a Zaire ebolavirus outbreak of equivalent scale.

The WHO EC process adds formal international coordination layers under IHR (2005) — this is the legal and diplomatic framework that obligates member states to coordinate border measures, report case data, and accept recommended resource-sharing. The 2014-2016 West Africa outbreak prompted the strongest reforms to this framework.
