# Ebola Outbreak Escalates — WHO Raises DRC Risk to 'Very High'

## What Happened

The WHO has upgraded its risk assessment for the Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of Congo to "very high" at the regional level (global risk remains "low"). Suspected cases have tripled in one week, raising alarms about the pace of spread. The outbreak has now killed more than 130 people with 600+ suspected cases.

Experts quoted in The Guardian note the US has "simply chosen not to stop" the outbreak following its massive public health funding cuts, creating a significant response gap.

## Why It Matters

- **First real test of post-US-funding WHO response**: Ebola Bundibugyo strain + WHO emergency declaration + US public health cuts = structural gap between "declaration" and "response capacity" now exposed at scale
- **UK vaccine development**: UK teams have been developing a new vaccine candidate, but rollout timelines remain unclear and likely too slow for current surge
- **Regional spread risk**: DRC's borders with Uganda, South Sudan, and Rwanda create transmission pathways; the outbreak is now in an active conflict zone where health infrastructure has been damaged
- **International Health Regulations trigger**: WHO "very high" rating typically triggers enhanced border screening and travel advisories from neighboring countries
