# A Foundational Overview of Biosemiotics

A comprehensive essay presenting biosemiotics as an ontological departure from the mechanical paradigm in biology. Draws on Peircean semiotics, von Uexküll's Umwelt theory, and Lotman's Semiosphere to argue that life is fundamentally a system of sign interpretation rather than mere chemical reaction.

## Key Concepts

- **Biological Paradigm Shift**: Biosemiotics proposes a new paradigm treating organisms as interpreters, not automata. Life is a relational system of meaning, not just thermodynamic necessity.
- **Peircean Triad vs. Saussurean Dyad**: Peirce's triadic model (Representamen → Object → Interpretant) is foundational for biology because it accounts for the agent and the "proper significate effect" (physiological response).
- **Organism as Autonomous Agent**: Stuart Kauffman's framing — organisms weave their own existence through self-interpretation. They are "self-reading texts" using organic codes to translate genetic instructions into physiological reality.
- **Umwelt (von Uexküll)**: The subjective semiotic world of an organism — a filtered reality constructed through species-specific sensors and needs. The environment is "diacritic" (a system of differences where only relevant signals are perceived).
- **Semiosphere (Lotman)**: The set of all interconnected Umwelts — the total semiotic space of nature. Implies nature is a site of knowledge, with non-human "knowers" engaged in constant information exchange.
- **Genetic Code as Writing**: Drawing on Derrida, the genotype is semiotic information while DNA is the material carrier. Protein synthesis is the "act of reading."
