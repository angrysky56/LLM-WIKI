# Menin, D-Serine, and Anti-Aging

Research exploring how the decline of **Menin** (a hypothalamic protein) and **D-serine** (a neurotransmitter amino acid) interact to drive biological aging, and how targeting these pathways might reverse age-related cognitive decline.

## Key Findings

### Menin's Role
Menin is a protein in the **hypothalamus** that suppresses neuroinflammation. As mice age, Menin levels in the ventromedial nucleus of the hypothalamus (VMH) drop sharply. This decline triggers:
- Increased brain inflammation
- Memory loss
- Thinning skin
- Bone loss
- Shortened lifespan

### The D-Serine Connection
- D-serine is an amino acid that acts as a neurotransmitter, critical for learning and memory
- Menin regulates the enzyme required to produce D-serine
- When Menin falls during aging, D-serine production drops proportionally
- This depletion directly causes cognitive impairment

### Reversing Decline

**Gene Therapy (experimental):** Restoring the Menin gene in elderly mice hypothalamus reversed aging signs including improved memory, balance, skin thickness, and bone density — while boosting D-serine. Not yet viable in humans.

**D-Serine Supplementation (practical):** Supplementing older mice with D-serine for 3 weeks improved cognitive performance and memory. However, it did **not** reverse physical aging markers (bone loss, skin thinning). This demonstrates that:
- D-serine addresses the cognitive/neurological symptoms of Menin loss
- Menin controls a broader, interconnected web of aging pathways beyond D-serine regulation

## Model
- Loss of Menin acts as a biological "aging switch" in the brain
- D-serine is one critical downstream effector of Menin signaling
- Targeting either Menin directly or D-serine indirectly could be promising therapies for age-related cognitive decline

## Caveats
- Research conducted in mice; human applicability unconfirmed
- The ScienceDaily source references a 2026-05-26 release, suggesting original paper likely from late May 2026
