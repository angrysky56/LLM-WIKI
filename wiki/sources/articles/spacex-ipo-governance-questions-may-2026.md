# SpaceX IPO — 16 Days Out: Governance Questions and Tesla Spillover Concerns

## Summary

With the SpaceX IPO now 16 days from its June 12 Nasdaq listing, two distinct governance concerns surfaced this cycle that weren't present in earlier coverage: (1) whether Musk's ~78% voting control structure will persist post-IPO and whether SpaceX will address this publicly before listing, and (2) whether SpaceX's $1.75 trillion valuation will trigger a Tesla re-rating as investor capital and attention shift.

## Key Developments This Cycle

**Barron's (May 26):** "Elon Musk's Bugs Bunny Moment: Will SpaceX IPO Tank Tesla Stock?" — Opens the question of Musk concentration risk. Frames Musk as a "Bugs Bunny" character who always "retires" but never follows through, questioning whether the IPO creates a zero-sum attention/capital dynamic between SpaceX and Tesla.

**24/7 Wall St. (May 26):** "The Question About Elon Musk That SpaceX Refuses to Answer Before Its IPO" — Identifies a specific governance gap: SpaceX has declined to clarify whether Musk's current voting control structure (approximately 78% voting control) will be maintained post-IPO, creating a governance asymmetry unusual for a public company of this size.

## Why This Matters

The governance question is the key institutional investor concern. Standard public-company governance norms expect some alignment between economic ownership and voting control. A 78%-controlled public company with no disclosure obligation on this structure is a novel instrument at this valuation scale. The Tesla spillover question is secondary but significant — if Tesla investors interpret SpaceX as Musk's primary attention post-IPO, Tesla could face a re-rating independent of SpaceX's own performance.
