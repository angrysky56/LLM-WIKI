# Ofcom: TikTok and YouTube "Not Safe Enough" for Children

## What Happened

The UK's communications regulator, Ofcom, has issued a finding that both TikTok and YouTube are "not safe enough" for children. The report assessed platform safety measures and found that neither company has done enough to protect minors from harmful content, algorithmic recommendation systems that can lead to dangerous content, and design features that may encourage addictive usage patterns.

TikTok said it was disappointed Ofcom had not acknowledged its safety features. YouTube said it worked with experts to provide appropriate experiences. Both platforms are under increasing regulatory pressure in the UK and EU, where the Online Safety Act is being operationalized.

## Why It Matters

This is part of a global pattern of children-focused social media regulation accelerating. In the US, Meta just settled a social media addiction case with a school district as a test case for 1,200+ similar suits. The convergence of US litigation and UK regulatory action creates a multi-front legal challenge for social media platforms.

The UK Online Safety Act represents one of the world's most comprehensive attempts to regulate platforms' duty of care to children. Ofcom's findings — and the platforms' pushback — will set precedent for how that law is interpreted and enforced.
