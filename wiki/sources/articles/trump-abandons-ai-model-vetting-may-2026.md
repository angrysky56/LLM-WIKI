# Trump Abandons AI Model Vetting Order Before Signing

## What Happened

The Trump administration **canceled the signing of an executive order** that would have granted the federal government authority to review AI models before their public release, according to the New York Times (May 22, 2026). The order had been under consideration for pre-release government vetting of AI systems developed by major US labs including Google, Microsoft, and xAI.

This comes **three days after** the May 19 confirmation that Trump's original AI executive order — which was framed as establishing a federal AI regulatory framework — was abandoned.

## Why It Matters

The cancellation is more significant than the proposal itself. It marks the **second reversal in one week** on federal AI governance:

1. **May 19**: Federal AI regulation officially off the table — the original executive order framework was abandoned
2. **May 22**: Even the alternative mechanism (pre-release model vetting) was cancelled before signing

This creates a governance vacuum that California is actively filling — the statewide AI worker protection order from May 21 positioned California as the *de facto* US AI regulator. The EU is simultaneously pressing its own AI Act implementation, creating a transatlantic divergence where companies face fundamentally different compliance frameworks on each side of the Atlantic.

## Caveats

The NY Times article should be read carefully for what exactly was being proposed vs. what was in the original executive order framework. The administration may still pursue sector-specific AI guidance through existing agencies (FDA for medical AI, FTC for consumer AI) without a centralized pre-release vetting regime.
