# China Defends Cuba Against US "Threats" — May 2026

## What Happened

China's foreign ministry publicly called on the United States to end its "threats" against Cuba, following the Trump DOJ's indictment of former Cuban President Raúl Castro. The charges against Castro represent a significant escalation in US pressure on Cuba — and have drawn a direct response from Beijing.

Cuba is currently experiencing widespread blackouts, and word of the US indictment has spread slowly through the island amid the power crisis. Neighbors of top Cuban officials report growing fear of potential US military action.

The timing is significant: Xi Jinping and Putin were meeting in Beijing just as the Cuba charges became public, in what was described as a rare display of alliance between the two powers.

## Why It Matters

This is a direct great-power signalling event. China publicly backing Cuba against US "threats" is the kind of public alignment that rarely happens — it signals a willingness to use Cuba as a node in the broader US-China rivalry. The framing ("threats" against a sovereign state) is also a rebuttal to US positioning on the Iran war and trade tensions.

The institutional capture vs species framing applies here: the rhetorical layer says "China defends small nation against bully." The operational layer says: China is using Cuba as a proxy signal point in a multipolar realignment.

The blackouts add a human dimension — Cubans are experiencing infrastructure stress at the same time their government is under US legal pressure. This creates a compounding crisis that Beijing may use to position itself as a stabilizer.
