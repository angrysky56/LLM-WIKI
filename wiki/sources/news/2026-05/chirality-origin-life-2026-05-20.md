# Chirality Origin Mystery — Why Life's Molecules Are "Right-Handed" ⚛️

## What Happened

Scientists have published research pointing to an elusive electronic effect that may finally explain why most biological molecules — amino acids, sugars — are "right-handed" (a property called homochirality). This has been one of the deepest unsolved problems in abiogenesis for 150 years.

The handedness of biological molecules is pervasive: every amino acid in your body is left-handed, every sugar is right-handed. But there's no fundamental physical law requiring this — it's a symmetry-breaking event in early evolution that locked in one chirality over the other.

## Why It Matters

The origin of homochirality is one of the key transitions from chemistry to biology — the point where non-living chemistry started producing molecules that could only exist in one "handedness," which is a prerequisite for the complex molecular recognition that life requires (e.g., enzymes fitting their substrates like a lock and key).

This connects to several wiki threads:
- **Entropic machinery**: The maintenance of chirality is itself a form of information preservation against racemization (the tendency to go to a 50/50 mixture). Life's molecular machinery actively resists entropy at the chiral level.
- **Molecular self**: The three-bond structure from Chen et al.'s molecular CoT research has a structural analogy to chirality — covalent bonds are directionally asymmetric in space.
- **Open-ended evolution**: Homochirality as a symmetry-breaking event that enabled unlimited complexity is conceptually analogous to the state-splitting events in MOP/EDM.

The fact that the solution involves an "elusive electronic effect" suggests this is a quantum-level phenomenon — which would make it one of the deepest layers of explanation in the entropic machinery thread.
