# Air France Flight Diverted to Montreal — Ebola Travel Restrictions in Action 🦠

## What Happened

An Air France flight destined for the United States was diverted to Montreal after a passenger from the Democratic Republic of Congo — the center of the ongoing Ebola outbreak — boarded "in error." The passenger was detected and the plane diverted to Canada amid Ebola-related travel restrictions.

This is the first documented case of Ebola-related travel restrictions causing a major international flight diversion. US-bound flights are now screening passengers from affected regions, and the incident shows how the WHO's international emergency declaration (PHEIC) is being operationalized at the border level.

## Why It Matters

The DRC Ebola outbreak has already caused 600+ suspected cases and 139 deaths according to WHO reporting as of May 20. WHO declared it a Public Health Emergency of International Concern (PHEIC) — the highest alert tier. This Air France diversion is the first visible evidence of that declaration changing real-world travel infrastructure.

The phrase "in error" is doing a lot of work here — it suggests either a ticketing system failure or a failure of pre-screening protocols. Either way, the fact that someone from an active Ebola hotspot region was able to board a transatlantic flight highlights the porosity of travel screening systems.

The broader implication: Ebola is no longer just a health story. It's becoming a travel, trade, and economic story — which is exactly how global health crises become geopolitical events.
