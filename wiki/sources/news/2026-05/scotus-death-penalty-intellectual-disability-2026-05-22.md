# Supreme Court Bars Execution of Intellectually Disabled Inmate — 2026-05-21

## What Happened

The US Supreme Court issued a landmark ruling in *Hamm v. Smith* on May 21, 2026, narrowly barring the execution of an inmate who may be intellectually disabled. The 5-4 decision found that executing such an inmate would violate the Eighth Amendment's prohibition on cruel and unusual punishment. Notably, conservative Justices Barrett and Kavanaugh joined the four Democratic appointees in the majority.

## Why It Matters

This is the first Supreme Court case involving intellectual disability and capital punishment since *Atkins v. Virginia* (2002), which established that executing people with intellectual disabilities is unconstitutional. The new case addressed a gap in *Atkins*: what happens when factual evidence of disability emerges or is updated after sentencing. The Court's ruling clarifies that post-conviction evidence of intellectual disability can be sufficient to bar execution.

The decision also signals that even in a politically polarized environment, some Eighth Amendment protections retain cross-ideological support. The Barrett-Kavanaugh alignment with the liberal wing on this specific issue is notable.
