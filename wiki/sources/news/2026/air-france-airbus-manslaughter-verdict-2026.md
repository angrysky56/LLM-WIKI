# Air France and Airbus Found Guilty of Manslaughter Over 2009 Crash

## What Happened

A French court has found Air France and Airbus guilty of manslaughter over the 2009 Air France Flight 447 crash that killed 228 people. The plane crashed over the Atlantic Ocean after encountering icing conditions that caused the pitot tubes (air speed sensors) to fail, leading to a loss of control. The verdict holds the companies criminally liable for the deaths.

The case has been in litigation for 17 years, with prosecutors arguing that Airbus and Air France failed to take adequate safety measures given known risks with the pitot tube design and the delay in implementing a fix for the icing issue.

## Why It Matters

This is a significant verdict for several reasons:

1. **Corporate criminal liability**: Finding a company guilty of manslaughter for a design flaw is rare and sets an important precedent for aviation safety accountability.

2. **Aviation industry impact**: The case puts manufacturers on notice that known safety issues must be addressed promptly, not deferred for cost or operational reasons.

3. **Global reach**: French courts have jurisdiction because the crash involved a French-registered airline and a French-manufactured aircraft, but the precedent will be cited in other jurisdictions.
