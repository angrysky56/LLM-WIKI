# Ebola Bundibugyo Outbreak — DRC and Uganda, 2026

## What Happened

The WHO declared the Bundibugyo Ebola virus outbreak in the Democratic Republic of Congo and Uganda a public health emergency of international concern approximately 6 days ago (around May 16, 2026). This is the first WHO emergency declaration for this particular species of Ebola, which has no proven vaccine. Bundibugyo has a case fatality rate of around 37%.

UK scientists are now developing a new vaccine specifically targeting the Bundibugyo species, with estimates it could be ready for trials within months. Health experts convened to discuss vaccine and treatment options. The outbreak has been surging, with scientists racing to develop drugs and vaccines.

Critically, experts are pointing to US public health cuts as significantly impairing the international response. The US had been a major funder of outbreak surveillance and response infrastructure; those cuts are now being felt directly in the DRC/Uganda context.

## Why It Matters

This is a test case for whether global pandemic response infrastructure can function when major traditional funders are withdrawing support. The WHO declaration — the highest alert level — signals international concern, but declarations without funded responses are essentially performative.

The lack of a proven vaccine for Bundibugyo is itself notable. Unlike the Zaire species (which has multiple effective vaccines), Bundibugyo has been relatively neglected in vaccine research, partly because past outbreaks were smaller and more contained. The current outbreak is changing that calculus.

The combination of: (1) no existing vaccine, (2) a growing case count, (3) response infrastructure under strain, and (4) community trust issues (based on carryover from prior cycle's hospital tent fire incident) creates compound risk.
