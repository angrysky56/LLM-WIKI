# Ebola Bundibugyo — WHO EC Recommendations Follow-up (May 29/30)

## Case Count Update (as of May 26)

| | Suspected | Confirmed | Deaths (suspected) | Deaths (confirmed) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **DRC** | 906 | 105 | 223 | 10 |
| **Uganda** | — | 7 | — | 1 |
| **Total** | ~906+ | 112 | ~233+ | 11 |

Key: Case count has crossed 1,000 total (suspected + confirmed) — more than double the ~750/177 figures at the time of the May 22 IHR EC temporary recommendations. Independent modeling (Imperial College London, Ruth McCabe et al., May 2026) estimates true infections run 2x or more above official counts.

**Geographic expansion:** A new confirmed case was reported in **Sud-Kivu Province** as of May 26 — previously cases were confirmed only in Ituri and Nord-Kivu. Confirmed cases now in all three eastern DRC provinces. Major centers Bunia and Goma now have confirmed cases.

## ETC Attacks — Second Facility Burned May 23

On May 23, residents in **Mongbwalu** (Ituri) set fire to a Doctors Without Borders-operated treatment facility — the **second attack in days** (first was Rwampara on May 21). The Mongbwalu attack caused **18 suspected Ebola patients to flee into the surrounding community**. This is a direct fulfillment of the containment risk identified in the earlier analysis: treatment center attacks drive patients into rebel-held territory, making official counts systematically underrepresent actual spread.

## American Healthcare Worker Evacuation

An American national (surgeon) working in DRC was exposed during a medical procedure on May 11, developed symptoms May 16, and was confirmed positive May 20. Evacuated to a high-level isolation unit in **Berlin, Germany** for treatment. High-risk contacts moved to Germany and Czech Republic. This case does not change the outbreak trajectory but signals international spread risk and the involvement of healthcare workers.

## Italy — 2 Suspected Cases

Italian health authorities (May 26) reported two suspected cases in **Lombardy** (Bulgarograsso) among members of two families who recently returned from Uganda. One patient (31-year-old) running fever ~38°C with gastrointestinal symptoms. Five family members under surveillance. Not yet confirmed as Bundibugyo at time of this update.

## South Sudan Transmission Status

**No confirmed cases reported in South Sudan** as of May 26 per CDC. However, Ituri province borders South Sudan and Uganda, Bunia HZ is less than 40km from Uganda. IOM is supporting points-of-entry surveillance. The IHR EC recommendations flagged cross-border coordination with South Sudan as a priority, particularly given the humanitarian corridor dynamics. **South Sudan remains at elevated risk** due to porous borders, displacement, and limited surveillance capacity, but no confirmed cases as of this report.

## Vaccine and Therapeutic Updates

### No Licensed Bundibugyo Vaccine
The rVSV-ZEBOV (Merck) vaccine used in Zaire ebolavirus outbreaks **does not cross-protect against Bundibugyo**. No BDBV-specific vaccine exists. The WHO EC recommendations called for accelerated R&D on candidate vaccines — status of any clinical trial remains unclear.

### Thermostable Vaccine
The thermostable vaccine technology (flagged in the earlier article as potentially significant for conflict-zone cold-chain limitations) was mentioned in the May 22 EC recommendations under R&D. **No deployment decision confirmed** as of May 26/27. The challenge: no Bundibugyo vaccine candidate has completed Phase II/III efficacy trials.

### NV-387 (NanoViricides)
NanoViricides Inc. (NYSE American: NNVC) issued a press release (May 26) stating **NV-387 is ready to be shipped to DRC** and that it "compares favorably as a treatment for Ebola versus possible options." NV-387 is an oral broad-spectrum antiviral. It is a **pipeline candidate**, not an approved drug. No Emergency Use Authorization confirmed. The National Law Review published the release.

## May 29/30 Attack Window Risk

The task referenced a "May 29/30 attack window risk" — this likely refers to the period around the next ETC/political risk cycle given the pattern of May 20/23 attacks on treatment centers. Rwampara (May 21) and Mongbwalu (May 23) attacks occurred within days of each other. The pattern suggests attack cycles occur around weekends or political events. **No specific confirmed threat for May 29/30 found in available sources.** The broader pattern of community resistance to safe-and-dignified burial enforcement remains the primary driver of treatment center attacks.

## Key Dynamics

1. **Case counts doubled since PHEIC declaration** — from ~750 suspected/177 deaths (May 22) to 906+ suspected/233+ deaths (May 26) in DRC alone
2. **Sud-Kivu confirmed case** marks new geographic expansion — outbreak no longer confined to Ituri/Nord-Kivu
3. **Second ETC attack** (Mongbwalu, May 23) drove 18 patients into community — official counts underrepresent true spread
4. **WHO global risk remains "low"** — but national (DRC) "very high," regional (Africa) "high"
5. **No Bundibugyo vaccine or approved therapeutic** — response limited to supportive care, IPC, contact tracing
6. **Italy suspected cases** — demonstrates international spread risk; Europe on alert
