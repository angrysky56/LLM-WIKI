**Summary**: US health officials have warned that the ongoing Ebola outbreak in central Africa could match the scale of the 2014 West Africa epidemic, which killed over 11,000 people. The outbreak, centered in the Democratic Republic of the Congo and with confirmed cases in Uganda, has prompted concerns about international containment capacity.

## Key Details

- **2014 comparison**: US health officials stated the current outbreak trajectory could match the 2014 record outbreak if containment measures are not rapidly scaled (The Guardian).
- **Kenya quarantine controversy**: An American-only Ebola quarantine centre plan in Kenya has drawn criticism from global health experts as discriminatory and counterproductive (The Guardian).
- **WHO assessment**: The World Health Organization has been monitoring the outbreak since its declaration as a Public Health Emergency of International Concern (PHEIC) earlier this year.
- **Regional spread**: Cases confirmed in DRC and Uganda, with concerns about cross-border transmission.

## Significance

The 2014 West Africa Ebola epidemic was the largest in history, overwhelming healthcare systems across Guinea, Liberia, and Sierra Leone. A match of that scale in central Africa would present even greater logistical challenges given the region's limited healthcare infrastructure and ongoing conflict in eastern DRC.
