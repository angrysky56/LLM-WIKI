# Ebola Outbreak in DRC and Uganda — PHEIC Declared (May 2026)

## What Happened

The World Health Organization declared the Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of the Congo and Uganda a **Public Health Emergency of International Concern (PHEIC)** on May 18, 2026. The outbreak involves the **Bundibugyo strain** of the Ebola virus — distinct from the Zaire strain that caused prior major outbreaks.

**Key metrics (as of May 22):**
- ~750 suspected cases across DRC and Uganda
- 177 deaths attributed to the outbreak
- Cases tripling week-over-week
- WHO risk assessment: "very high" at regional level
- 10 African countries flagged as at-risk by Africa CDC

## Why This Is Different

1. **Bundibugyo strain**: Less studied than Zaire, no proven vaccine for this specific strain
2. **No existing vaccine**: The current outbreak response relies on experimental vaccines — no licensed option for Bundibugyo
3. **Funding cuts caused delayed detection**: The International Rescue Committee reports that US funding withdrawals led to delayed detection — outbreak started weeks before international recognition
4. **Treatment centers targeted**: A second Ebola treatment center was set ablaze in eastern Congo; 18 suspected cases fled
5. **US doctor infected**: A US doctor was confirmed infected — CDC implementing enhanced screening at Washington-Dulles for US citizens returning from Uganda

## Response Landscape

- **US response gap operational**: Experts publicly stating "US is choosing not to stop" the outbreak —撤离 detainee removals to DRC paused by White House
- **UK vaccine candidate in development** but too slow for current surge
- **DRC province banned funeral wakes** to contain spread through burial rites
- **MONUSCO (UN peacekeeping force)** supporting response in DRC
- **CDC enhanced screening** at US airports for travelers from affected regions
