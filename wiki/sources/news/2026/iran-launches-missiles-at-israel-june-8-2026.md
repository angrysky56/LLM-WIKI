# Iran Launches Missiles at Israel — Direct Escalation After Beirut Strike

**Date**: June 8, 2026  
**Sources**: BBC, New York Times, Al Jazeera, Bloomberg  
**Significance**: Iran launched missiles at Israel on June 8, the first direct state-to-state exchange since an April 2026 ceasefire. The strikes come days after Israel targeted Beirut, which Iran described as having "crossed all red lines." Debris was reported in Jordan from intercepted missiles. Iran's stated aim is to restore deterrence while avoiding a full return to war.

## What Happened

- Iran fired multiple ballistic missiles at Israel, with some intercepted over Jordanian airspace where debris was reported
- Explosions reported in Tehran and Isfahan as Israel launched retaliatory strikes on Iranian military infrastructure
- Iran's leadership described the attacks as a calibrated response to Israel's Beirut strike — aiming to restore deterrence "without triggering a wider war"
- Israel's security cabinet convened in emergency session; the IDF stated it had "prepared response options across the region"

## Significance

This is the most direct Iran-Israel military confrontation since the April ceasefire. The escalation chain: Israel bombed Beirut (June 7) → Iran vowed retaliation → missiles launched within 24 hours. The risk of a wider regional war — involving Hezbollah, Iranian proxies in Syria/Iraq, and potentially Gulf state airspace — is the highest it has been since the April crisis.
