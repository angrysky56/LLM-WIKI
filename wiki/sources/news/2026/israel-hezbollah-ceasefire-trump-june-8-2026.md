**Summary**: President Donald Trump announced that Israel and Hezbollah have agreed to "stop all shooting" following a weekend of missile exchanges that shattered the April ceasefire. Iran also signaled willingness to de-escalate after striking Israel on Saturday and facing Israeli retaliation. The development marks a rapid reversal from the previous 48 hours of escalation.

## Key Details

- **Ceasefire announcement**: Trump claimed Hezbollah and Israel agreed to cease hostilities, per The Guardian. Lebanon also announced a partial ceasefire between Israel and Hezbollah (Reuters).
- **Iran signals end**: Reports indicate Iran has ended strikes on Israel, with copper and gold markets rebounding on the news (Bloomberg).
- **UN Security Council**: Emergency session convened on Lebanon as Israeli attacks on Beirut suburbs continued before the ceasefire.
- **Market response**: European stocks erased losses, US stocks bounced, oil prices eased as de-escalation hopes emerged (Bloomberg).
- **Lebanon dynamics**: Lebanon emerged as a wedge issue between Trump and Netanyahu, per Bloomberg analysis.

## Significance

The speed of escalation-to-de-escalation (less than 72 hours) suggests both Iran and Hezbollah sought a calibrated response rather than all-out war. The Trump-mediated ceasefire represents a diplomatic win for the administration and potentially resets US-Israel dynamics. The UN Security Council emergency session indicates ongoing multilateral engagement.
