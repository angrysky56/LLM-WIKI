# Malaysia Exits US Reciprocal Trade Deal After Supreme Court Ruling

## What Happened

Malaysia has withdrawn from its reciprocal trade deal with the United States following the US Supreme Court's May 2026 ruling that invalidated aspects of the Trump administration's tariff authority, The New Indian Express reported (March 2026, confirmed in recent coverage).

The Malaysia-US trade deal had been negotiated as part of the administration's broader effort to secure bilateral trade agreements after imposing sweeping "Liberation Day" tariffs in April 2025. Malaysia's exit is a direct consequence of the Supreme Court's tariff ruling, which eliminated the legal foundation for the administration's preferred approach of using tariff threats as negotiation leverage.

## Why It Matters

1. **First country to formally exit a US bilateral trade deal post-ruling** — This sets a precedent for other countries considering whether to continue negotiating under a weakened US tariff posture.

2. **Supreme Court ruling ripple effects** — The ruling invalidated the 10% global tariff as illegal, forcing the administration to either seek congressional authorization or pursue bilateral deals from a position of reduced leverage.

3. **Southeast Asian trade architecture** — Malaysia's exit contributes to fragmentation in US-ASEAN trade relations, with implications for supply chains in electronics, semiconductors, and palm oil.

4. **Signals judicial constraints on executive trade authority** — Courts are now an active constraint on executive branch trade policy in a way that hadn't been visible before the May 2026 ruling.
