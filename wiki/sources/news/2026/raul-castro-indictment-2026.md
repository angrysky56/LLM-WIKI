## Core Insight

The US indictment of Raúl Castro is not primarily about justice for the 1996 plane shootdown — it's a pressure tactic in a broader coercive campaign. Russia and China recognise it as such, with the Kremlin explicitly saying the pressure "borders on violence." The indictment fits a pattern: US seizure of Maduro (January 2026) used as justification for the Caracas raid, now repeated targeting Cuba's former leader.

## Key Claims

| Claim | Detail |
|-------|--------|
| Indictment target | Raúl Castro (former president, head of armed forces in 1996) + 5 others |
| Charge | Murder — over 1996 downing of two Brothers to the Rescue planes; 4 dead (3 US citizens) |
| Penalties | Life imprisonment or death |
| Russia response | "Borders on violence" — cannot be condoned; Kremlin spokesman Peskov |
| China response | Called on US to stop "coercion" and "threats"; "firmly supports Cuba" |
| US pressure campaign | Fresh sanctions, effective blockade on oil shipments → fuel crisis, blackouts, food shortages |
| Cuba context | "Ready to fall" — Trump quote, January 2026 after Maduro capture |
| US Secretary of State | Marco Rubio — declined to comment on plans to bring Castro to US |

## Escalation Context

The indictment of a former head of state is extraordinary. The fact that Castro is now 93 and in poor health makes the death penalty exposure particularly notable. Russia and China framing this as "violence" raises the temperature on great power competition around the Caribbean.
