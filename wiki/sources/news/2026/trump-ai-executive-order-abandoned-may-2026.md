# Trump Administration Abandons AI Executive Order (May 22, 2026)

## What Happened

The Trump administration called off a proposed executive order that would have granted the federal government oversight of AI models before their release, according to reporting from The New Indian Express on May 22, 2026. The reversal came after objections from technology industry groups who argued such pre-release vetting would undermine US AI competitiveness relative to China.

The proposed order was first reported in early May 2026, with the White House apparently considering mandatory evaluation requirements for frontier AI models before public deployment. The reversal marks a sharp departure from even the limited regulatory approach contemplated just weeks earlier.

## Why It Was Abandoned

The tech industry's argument centered on competitive dynamics:
- **China competition frame**: Industry argued that pre-release government review would slow US AI development and give China an advantage in the global AI race
- **Innovation burden**: Mandatory evaluation requirements would add bureaucratic hurdles to AI development cycles
- **Classification concerns**: Some AI systems with national security implications might require government review, but broader application was seen as overreach

## Context: States vs. Federal Divergence

The Trump reversal amplifies a broader pattern of federal inaction on AI regulation while states act:

- **California** signed its own executive order (May 21) specifically to prepare workers and businesses for AI disruption — explicitly a proactive, state-level response
- **22 other states** have active AI bills in various legislative stages
- The federal AI policy vacuum is being filled by state-level initiatives

The White House has effectively signaled it will not pursue federal AI safety or evaluation requirements, creating a regulatory patchwork across states.

## Why It Matters

The abandoned federal AI order would have been the most significant US government intervention in AI development since the 2023-2024 AI executive orders from the Biden administration. Its abandonment under industry pressure signals that the current administration is unlikely to pursue binding AI safety regulations in the near term.

This creates a structural gap: states like California are moving forward with AI worker protections and evaluation requirements, while the federal government has effectively ceded AI regulatory authority — at least temporarily. The result is an increasingly fragmented AI policy landscape where compliance requirements vary by jurisdiction.

For the AI labs, this removes a potential compliance burden but also removes a layer of political cover — they can no longer point to federal requirements as constraints. State-level pressure is now the primary regulatory force on AI development practices.
