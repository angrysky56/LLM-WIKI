# US Proposes Sweeping Forced-Labor Tariffs on 60 Economies

**Date**: June 5, 2026  
**Sources**: NYT, WSJ, CNBC, WaPo, Reuters, ABC News  
**Significance**: Largest single trade action since Trump's 2018-2019 tariff wave; targets China, EU, Mexico, India, and 57 others.

## What Happened

The Trump administration proposed new tariffs of 10% or more on 59 countries and the European Union, citing forced labor trade practices after a USTR investigation (Reuters, CNBC). The move applies to most of America's largest trading partners simultaneously — China, the EU, Mexico, India, Japan, South Korea, Vietnam, and dozens more. The USTR cited forced labor findings from probes initiated earlier in the administration.

## Why It Matters

1. **Scale unprecedented**: The 2018 tariff wave targeted ~$250B in Chinese goods. This action covers virtually all US trading partners simultaneously. If implemented, it would be the broadest trade action in modern US history.

2. **Trade war 2.0**: Comes on top of existing US-China tariffs, Brazil 25% tariff (June 2), Canada 51st-state tension, and USMCA renewal negotiations. The US is now simultaneously in trade disputes with its three largest partners (China, EU, Mexico) plus dozens of others.

3. **Supply chain shock**: Companies that built diversified sourcing after 2018 now face tariffs on all their locations simultaneously. No jurisdiction is spared — the forced-labor rationale is universal.

4. **WTO implications**: The forced-labor rationale may be legally distinct from the Section 301/232 tariffs that the WTO already ruled against. The administration is building a new legal architecture for protectionism.

5. **Political timing**: Coincides with the Iran war, House war-powers vote fight, and pre-election positioning. Trump is consolidating his trade-war legacy across every front simultaneously.

- These are *proposed* tariffs under a notice-and-comment period; final rates and scope may change
- The forced-labor legal standard requires specific evidence per country; implementation may be uneven
- Multiple countries will file WTO challenges; domestic legal challenges also expected
