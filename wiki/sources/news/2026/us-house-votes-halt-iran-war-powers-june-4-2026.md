# US House Votes to Halt Trump Iran War — 215-208 War Powers Resolution

**Date**: 2026-06-03 (US time) / 2026-06-04 (publication)
**Significance**: 🔴 **TOP STORY** — First successful House check on presidential war authority since the February launch of the Iran war. Bipartisan. Pairs with Iran's June 3 strikes on Kuwait/Bahrain and Trump's same-day "we're close to a deal" messaging.

## What Happened

The US House of Representatives passed a war powers resolution **215-208** on Wednesday (2026-06-03) directing President Trump to either withdraw US forces from the Iran war or seek explicit congressional authorization. **Four Republicans** — Thomas Massie, Brian Fitzpatrick, Tom Barrett, and Warren Davidson — joined a unified Democratic caucus. Democrat Jared Golden (ME), a previous holdout, switched to support. This is the **fourth** House attempt to rein in Trump's war powers since the war began; the Senate advanced a similar resolution in May but has not held a full floor vote.

## Constitutional & Political Weight

- The measure is a **concurrent resolution** — if the Republican-controlled Senate also passes it, it does not require the President's signature, but it is subject to legal challenge.
- The **White House dismissed the merits** as "an unconstitutional attempt to restrict presidential power."
- Resolution lead sponsor **Gregory Meeks** (D-NY, ranking member of House Foreign Affairs): "a significant bipartisan rebuke of President Trump's illegal and costly war in Iran and the first step toward ending it once and for all."
- The vote follows a **prior intra-Republican revolt** that forced Trump to pull back a $1.8B "anti-weaponization" slush fund — meaning Republican defections on presidential authority are no longer one-off.
- Coincides with **public pressure** from rising petrol prices and polling against an open-ended war.

## Context: The Iran War in 2026

- **2026-02-28**: US and Israel launched strikes on Iran.
- Iran responded by attacking Israel and **US-allied Gulf states**, and effectively **closed the Strait of Hormuz**.
- **2026-04**: US imposed a naval blockade on Iran-bound shipping.
- **2026-04-08**: US-Iran reached an initial ceasefire (since broken repeatedly).
- **2026-06-01**: US bombed Iranian radar sites and air defenses; Iran struck Kuwait radar sites.
- **2026-06-02**: Trump claimed "expects deal over the next week"; partial Israel-Hezbollah ceasefire announced.
- **2026-06-03** (cycle before this): Iran struck Kuwait International Airport (1+ killed) and Bahrain; US and Iran accused each other; Rubio publicly said war is "over."
- **2026-06-03** (same day as this vote): Trump told reporters the US struck Iran "the night before" and that negotiations are close.

## Significance — Why This Matters

1. **Constitutional precedent**: This is the first successful House check on Trump's war authority since the war's February launch. If the Senate follows, it will be the first concurrent war powers resolution to reach the President's desk (where he can refuse to sign it but it still goes on record).
2. **Republican fracture widens**: Four GOP defections + the prior "anti-weaponization" fund pullback indicate the President's domestic political capital is no longer unbreakable on foreign-policy issues.
3. **Petrol-price economics** are now driving congressional behavior: the war's domestic cost is no longer abstract.
4. **Diplomacy-track signal**: The vote is being read as a green light for Trump to "land a deal" without losing face domestically — useful for both the Iran negotiation and the parallel Israel-Hezbollah track.

## Caveats

- Concurrent resolution legal weight is contested; White House has threatened legal challenge.
- Trump simultaneously told reporters he expects to close the deal "as soon as this weekend" — meaning this vote may be a face-saving structure, not a substantive pivot.
- A 215-208 vote is not a veto-proof majority; even if Senate passes, Trump could refuse to sign and the courts would decide.
