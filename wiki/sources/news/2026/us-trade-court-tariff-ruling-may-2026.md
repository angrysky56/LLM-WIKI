# US Trade Court Rules Trump 10% Global Tariff Illegal

## What Happened

A US trade court ruled on May 7, 2026 that President Trump's 10% global tariff is illegal, marking the first major legal setback to his trade war. The ruling found the administration exceeded its statutory authority under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act (IEEPA). The ruling does not immediately invalidate the tariffs but creates legal uncertainty that could reshape the trade war's trajectory.

The ruling came after Trump's announcement of new 10% global tariffs following a Supreme Court loss on the tariff issue — the President raged publicly about the court decision before announcing the new tariff tranche.

The US trade deficit grew in March 2026 as the tariff regime took effect, compounding economic pressures.

## Why It Matters

- **Legal precedent**: This is the first successful court challenge to Trump's sweeping tariff authority. It establishes that emergency economic powers cannot be used for routine trade protectionism.
- **Trade war trajectory**: The ruling creates a two-track system — some tariffs legally imposed (via congressional authority or trade agreements) vs. those challenged under IEEPA.
- **Market impact**: The ruling introduces significant uncertainty for businesses planning investment and supply chain decisions.
- **Appeal risk**: The administration will likely appeal, prolonging uncertainty and potentially reaching the Supreme Court.
