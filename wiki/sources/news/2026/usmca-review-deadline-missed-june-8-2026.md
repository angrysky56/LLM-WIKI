**Summary**: The United States, Mexico, and Canada will miss the July 2026 deadline for the scheduled USMCA review, ramping up trade tensions between the three nations. The missed deadline introduces uncertainty into the trilateral trade framework that governs over $1.5 trillion in annual North American trade.

## Key Details

- **Missed deadline**: The three countries will fail to meet the July USMCA review date, according to reports from Bloomberg and Claims Journal.
- **Trade tension**: The delay ramps up existing trade friction, with unresolved issues around digital trade, energy, and automotive rules of origin.
- **Context**: The Middle East crisis may be crowding out trade bandwidth, as noted in the previous cycle's carryover as a risk factor.
- **Market implications**: The missed deadline adds to the broader trade policy uncertainty that has weighed on North American markets.

## Significance

The USMCA review was designed to ensure the 2020 agreement remained current. Missing the July date raises questions about the durability of the framework and opens space for renegotiation pressure from all three parties. The delay compounds existing trade friction: US tariffs on Canadian lumber and Mexican agricultural goods remain unresolved, while the digital services tax dispute continues.
