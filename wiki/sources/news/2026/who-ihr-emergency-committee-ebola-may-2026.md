# WHO IHR Emergency Committee — First Meeting (May 22, 2026)

## What Happened

The WHO Director-General convened the first meeting of the IHR Emergency Committee regarding the Ebola Bundibugyo virus disease outbreak in the Democratic Republic of the Congo and Uganda on May 22, 2026. The Committee issued temporary recommendations under the International Health Regulations (2005).

The outbreak remains a Public Health Emergency of International Concern (PHEIC), declared May 17, 2026. The Emergency Committee's deliberations focused on:
- The cross-border transmission dynamics between DRC and Uganda
- Vaccine availability and trial acceleration
- Travel and trade restrictions being imposed by various nations
- Coordination gaps between national response efforts

## Key Developments Since PHEIC Declaration

### Case Count Update
- Total confirmed cases: **395+** across DRC and Uganda
- Deaths: **106+** (case fatality rate ~27%)
- Bundibugyo strain confirmed as the causative agent
- No licensed vaccine exists for this strain — clinical trials planned

### India-Africa Forum Summit Postponed
India postponed the India-Africa Forum Summit (originally scheduled for coming weeks) amid Ebola outbreak concerns, citing travel risks and diplomatic protocol. This is a significant diplomatic consequence of the PHEIC.

### India's Travel Advisory
India issued an advisory against non-essential travel to DRC, Uganda, and South Sudan — the three most at-risk nations. Karnataka state in India also issued a health alert following WHO guidelines.

### WHO Risk Assessment
WHO has raised its Bundibugyo Ebola risk assessment for DRC to **"Very High"** at the provincial level. PAHO (Pan-American Health Organization) has reinforced preparedness measures across the Americas region, anticipating potential imported cases.

### Barrick Gold Mine Screening
Barrick's Kibali gold mine (one of Africa's largest) in DRC has intensified Ebola screening at its operations — indicating corporate concern about operational continuity and workforce safety.

## Temporary Recommendations (Summary)

The Committee advised affected states to:
1. Accelerate ring vaccination campaigns with available (though unlicensed) vaccine stocks
2. Strengthen cross-border surveillance and contact tracing
3. Maintain essential health services in affected areas
4. Avoid unnecessary travel restrictions that impede response
5. Increase funding to WHO for emergency operations

The Committee noted that the outbreak's geographic spread and conflict-related access challenges in eastern DRC remain the primary concerns.

## Why It Matters

The Emergency Committee meeting signals formal international coordination is now active beyond the initial PHEIC declaration. The temporary recommendations set the legal and operational framework for how nations must respond under IHR — affecting travel, trade, and health resource allocation globally. The postponement of the India-Africa Forum Summit demonstrates the outbreak's diplomatic footprint beyond health.

The absence of a licensed Bundibugyo vaccine remains the critical vulnerability — all other major Ebola outbreaks (Zaire, Sudan strains) had vaccine options. Ring vaccination with experimental stocks is the only tool available.
