## Core Synthesis

A cluster of entities—including facts about Ebola's rapid spread, USAID funding cuts, community skepticism of the virus, and the lack of a vaccine—reveals a dangerous feedback loop in the DRC outbreak. The interaction is causal: **funding cuts → reduced public health infrastructure → increased community distrust → more rapid spread → further destabilization → even less vaccine access**. This is not a linear crisis but a systemic failure cascade where each factor amplifies the others.

## The Feedback Loop

```
USAID cuts → reduced health outreach → community skepticism ↑
                                              ↓
                           misinformation spreads → fewer people seek treatment
                                              ↓
                           outbreak grows → health system overwhelmed
                                              ↓
                           international response hampered → further distrust
```

## Implications

- Humanitarian funding cuts during active outbreaks create compounding rather than linear effects
- Community distrust and misinformation are not external factors but endogenous to the aid system's capacity
- Breaking any single link requires simultaneous intervention at multiple points
