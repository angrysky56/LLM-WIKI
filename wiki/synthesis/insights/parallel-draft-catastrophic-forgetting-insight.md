## Core Synthesis

Low-cost parallel draft model adaptation—using multiple decoding heads in a single forward pass to accelerate LLM inference—is not an isolated optimization trick but sits at a convergence of three broader concerns. The same structural mitigation of catastrophic forgetting that enables parallel drafting also underpins memory-to-skill distillation in LLM agents (where episodic experiences become reusable skills), and both rely on avoiding representational collapse during multi-task learning.

## Implications

- Draft-model architectures may double as a testbed for continual learning and agent skill acquisition
- Inference efficiency techniques and agent cognitive architectures share a deeper structural dependency than previously recognized
- Parallel decoding could serve as a proxy for studying representational dynamics in multi-task learning systems
