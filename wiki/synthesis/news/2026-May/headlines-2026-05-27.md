## 1. EU-US Trade Deal Ratified with 2029 Expiry

The EU Council and Parliament formally ratified the EU-US bilateral trade agreement on May 20, 2026. The deal includes a **2029 expiry on tariff safeguards** — creating a defined sunset window rather than indefinite arrangement. This converts last week's political agreement into binding bilateral architecture.

Context: The Supreme Court's May 2026 ruling invalidated unilateral US tariff authority, providing the legal basis for a structured bilateral deal. The EU ratified knowing the US no longer has the legal instrument for open-ended tariff escalation.

## 2. Trump Abandons AI Model Vetting Before Signing

Three days after confirming the original federal AI executive order was off the table, the Trump administration canceled a second proposal — pre-release vetting of AI models from major labs (Google, Microsoft, xAI) — **before it was even signed**. The NY Times reported the reversal May 22.

Federal AI regulation is now definitively off the table in the US. California, which issued its AI worker protection order May 21, is functioning as *de facto* US AI regulator. This creates a transatlantic governance divergence with the EU's AI Act implementation.

## 3. Google DeepMind Launches Gemini for Science

Google DeepMind released a full **AI-native research platform** that autonomously designs, executes, and interprets scientific experiments — not just analyzing human-designed experiments. Accompanied by Co-Scientist, a multi-agent AI research partner architecture.

This is the most concrete step toward autonomous scientific discovery to date. The simultaneous coverage of UC Irvine's DARPA-funded AI-math project and OpenAI o3's Erdős proof suggests AI is accelerating across all domains simultaneously.
