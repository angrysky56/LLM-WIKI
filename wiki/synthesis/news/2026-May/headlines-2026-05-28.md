## 1. Ebola PHEIC — WHO Emergency Committee Issues Temporary Recommendations (May 22)

The WHO's IHR (International Health Regulations) Emergency Committee convened on May 22 and issued temporary recommendations for the Ebola Bundibugyo outbreak in DRC and Uganda — the first formal international governance escalation since the PHEIC was declared. This is the structure that carryover item "WHO Emergency Committee additional recommendations" was tracking, and it has now materialized.

**Key facts this cycle:**
- **Strain:** Bundibugyo Ebola — rare, no approved treatment. NanoViricides' oral NV-387 flagged as pipeline candidate
- **WHO Director-General** (May 22 media briefing): outbreak is "outpacing us" — 220+ suspected deaths at time of statement
- **Risk level upgraded** to "very high" for the affected area
- **~750 suspected cases, 177 deaths** at time of the CIDRAP report (May 22)
- WHO EC issued temporary recommendations (document published May 22)
- **Thermostable vaccine technology** flagged for deployment — potentially game-changing for conflict-zone logistics
- IRC (May 26): spreading faster than response capacity

**Context:** The Bundibugyo strain has no treatment, unlike Zaire ebolavirus. The outbreak is in active conflict zones in eastern DRC with 18+ patients fled into rebel territory after ETC attacks. The WHO EC process adds a formal international governance layer — it recommends coordinated border monitoring, vaccine sharing, and resource mobilization. This is now the most operationally active Ebola PHEIC since 2014-2016 West Africa, measured by international coordination mechanisms triggered.

## 2. SpaceX IPO — 16 Days Out, Governance Questions and Tesla Spillover Warnings

**Barron's (May 26):** "Will SpaceX IPO Tank Tesla Stock?" — opens the question of whether Musk's attention and capital will shift post-IPO, and whether SpaceX's $1.75T valuation creates a re-rating risk for Tesla. The article frames this as Musk's "Bugs Bunny moment" — the cartoon character who's always about to "retire" but never does.

**Why this matters:** This is the largest IPO in history by valuation. The governance question (Musk control, Musk attention, Musk capital allocation) is the key institutional investor concern and hasn't been resolved publicly.

## 3. Pope Leo XIV — First Encyclical "Magnifica humanitas" Demands Global AI Regulation

Pope Leo XIV released his first major theological text — the encyclical *Magnifica humanitas* — on May 26, making it the most significant statement by a global religious leader on AI governance to date.

**Key content:**
- **"AI must be disarmed"** — the framing is explicitly security/safety oriented, not just ethical
- AI must serve humanity, not concentrate power
- Calls for strict binding rules on AI companies
- Explicitly frames AI development as a form of arms race requiring disarmament-style treaties

**Responses this cycle:**
- Anthropic co-founder Chris Olah published remarks on the encyclical (May 25)
- Vatican press conference: "AI must be disarmed for humanity's sake"
- Multiple Catholic publications ran analysis pieces (May 26)

**Context:** This is a new voice in the global AI governance debate. The Pope has no direct regulatory authority, but *Magnifica humanitas* adds theological weight to the EU/regulatory-approach camp. It amplifies the narrative that AI development is a safety/arms-control problem, not just an innovation/commercial competition problem. Given the 1.4 billion Catholic population and the Vatican's diplomatic network, this increases pressure on governments that have been reluctant to regulate.

**Why this matters:** It inserts the arms-control framing into mainstream global political discourse at the highest institutional level. This is qualitatively different from corporate self-regulation proposals or government-only frameworks.
