# South Sudan at Breaking Point — May 2026

## The Situation

South Sudan is experiencing a significant escalation in violence. MSF (Doctors Without Borders) reports that civilians are being killed while fleeing attacks — indicating deliberate targeting of civilians in retreat. The violence includes mass rape, attacks on healthcare infrastructure, and use of hunger as a weapon of war.

This is occurring simultaneously with the Ebola outbreak in the DRC and Uganda. While South Sudan is not yet a primary outbreak zone, the convergence of violence and epidemic creates compound humanitarian crises.

## Why This Matters for the Ebola Response

The carryover noted South Sudan border status as an open question. The violence context is critical:

1. **Population displacement toward conflict zones**: Violence in South Sudan pushes civilians toward Ebola-affected regions or creates refugee flows that cross borders — complicating contact tracing and containment
2. **Healthcare system degradation**: Attacks on healthcare facilities reduce the capacity to respond to any Ebola cases that might emerge in South Sudan
3. **Cross-border transmission risk**: If Ebola reaches South Sudan's conflict zones, the combination of violence and epidemic would be catastrophic and nearly impossible to contain
4. **WHO monitoring gap**: Conflict zones are exactly where official case counts lag reality — the pattern seen in eastern DRC (rebel-held areas unreported) applies equally here

## The Broader Pattern: Hunger as Weapon

The Guardian (May 24) and multiple NGOs report that food is being weaponized in the conflict. This is not incidental — it's deliberate strategy. When hunger is used as a weapon, it creates conditions where:
- People migrate to find food, spreading both conflict and epidemic risk
- Malnutrition weakens immune systems, increasing Ebola transmission
- Healthcare workers cannot access populations

## UN Joint Declaration (May 12)

The African Commission on Human and Peoples' Rights and the UN Independent International Fact-Finding Mission for the Sudan adopted a joint declaration on the human rights situation. This signals the international community is increasing attention on Sudan — but declarations have limited effect on the ground when violence is deliberate and systematic.
