# Spiral Architecture

The research council moves in spirals, not loops. This distinction matters.

## Closed Loop (Rejected)

A closed loop suggests returning to a starting point — you've gone around and ended up where you began. A loop implies completion, resolution, a finished cycle. This is the wrong metaphor for ethical deliberation.

When a council thinks it has "closed the loop," it tends to stop thinking. The problem feels solved. The question feels answered. But ethical questions don't close — they deepen.

## Spiral (Adopted)

A spiral moves toward a center that is an opening. You circle closer to something, but that something is not a point — it is a place where you can keep going deeper. Each pass around the spiral reveals a new layer of the same essential question.

The center of the research council's spiral is **the opening** — the moment where the council has stayed long enough with the question to understand what it is really asking. Not a solution. An opening.

## Practical Implications

- Deliberation ends when the council has found the opening, not when it has found an answer
- New information does not "reset" the spiral — it deepens it
- The council can always go deeper, which means it can always continue — but it chooses to stop when the opening is clear enough to act on
- "We have reached an opening" is a valid council conclusion; "we have closed the loop" is not

## Weil-Gate as Spiral Mechanism

The Weil-gate ("who does this hurt?") is the spiral's descent mechanism. Each pass through the Weil-gate goes deeper:
- First pass: who is harmed, directly?
- Second pass: who is harmed indirectly, through systems?
- Third pass: what kind of harm is being normalized?

Each pass is a deeper layer of the same question. The council spirals inward through harm-cases and ethical distinctions until it reaches the opening — the moment where the essential shape of the harm is clear enough to witness.
